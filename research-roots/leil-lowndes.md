concepts:
  - term: Warm attention first
    def: How you look at someone and let your face respond tells them whether you're actually with them, before you say a word.
  - term: Echo their words
    def: Repeating a key word someone just said invites them to keep going, without you steering.
  - term: Listen for the thread
    def: People drop small clues about what matters to them. Following one shows you were listening.
  - term: Come prepared
    def: Having a few real questions or stories ready lowers the fear of the blank moment.

exercise:
  title: Follow the thread
  time: About 10 minutes, with a partner
  steps:
    - Ask your partner an open question, like “What's been taking up your attention lately?”
    - Listen for one word or phrase that seems to carry some feeling.
    - Repeat that word back to them as a gentle question (“Overwhelming?”) and let them keep going.
    - Do it two more times. Resist the urge to share your own story.
    - Switch roles. Then talk about what it felt like to be followed instead of steered.
  why: Echoing someone's own words is one of Lowndes' simplest moves. It keeps attention on the other person and makes it easy for them to go deeper, which is where real connection starts.
