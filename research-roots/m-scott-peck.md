title: Love as Will
people: M. Scott Peck (1936–2005)
field: Psychiatry · Spiritual growth · Community
tagline: Love is the effort you choose after the feeling fades.
who:
  - "M. Scott Peck practiced psychiatry, including in the U.S. Army, before *The Road Less Traveled* (1978) opened with the words “Life is difficult” and became one of the longest-running bestsellers of its era. He defined love as an act of will, not a feeling: choosing to extend yourself to nurture your own or another person’s spiritual growth."
  - "In *The Different Drum* (1987) he turned to groups and mapped four stages of community: pseudocommunity, chaos, emptiness and true community. He co-founded the Foundation for Community Encouragement to teach the process."
concepts:
  - term: Love as will
    def: Love is a choice to extend yourself for growth, yours or another’s.
  - term: Falling in love is not love
    def: The early feeling is real, but it isn’t yet the work of love.
  - term: Discipline
    def: Delaying gratification, accepting responsibility, dedication to truth.
  - term: Stages of community
    def: Pseudocommunity, chaos, emptiness, then real community.
context: "Peck’s definition of love, which bell hooks built on, is one of three converging definitions at the center of the labs, with hooks and Aquinas. His stages of community also describe what happens in a good group: politeness, then friction, then letting go, then real connection."
exercise:
  title: Extend yourself
  time: Over a week
  steps:
    - Choose one person whose growth you care about.
    - "Ask: what would genuinely help them grow right now? Not what would make them like you, but what would help them."
    - "Choose one act that takes real effort from you: time, attention, an honest conversation."
    - Do it this week.
    - Then ask the same question about yourself, and do one thing for your own growth too.
  why: Peck defined love as effort toward growth. Choosing an action by that standard, rather than by what feels nice, puts his definition into practice.
