title: The Shadow and the Whole Self
people: Carl Gustav Jung (1875–1961)
field: Analytical psychology
tagline: What we refuse to see in ourselves, we keep meeting in other people.
who:
  - "Carl Jung was a Swiss psychiatrist and the founder of analytical psychology. After an early collaboration with Sigmund Freud, he broke away to develop his own map of the psyche: the personal unconscious, the collective unconscious and its archetypes, and the lifelong process he called individuation."
  - "His work shaped Joseph Campbell’s hero’s journey, Clarissa Pinkola Estés’s story medicine and Maureen Murdock’s heroine’s journey, and it echoes in parts work like Internal Family Systems. Most of his ideas come from clinical observation, dreams and comparative myth rather than controlled studies. We treat them as a working map, not proven science."
concepts:
  - term: The shadow
    def: The parts of ourselves we disown because they didn’t feel safe or acceptable. Unseen, they run us from the side.
  - term: Projection
    def: Seeing our own disowned qualities in someone else, and reacting to them there.
  - term: Archetypes
    def: Recurring patterns and figures, like the hero, the mother or the trickster, that show up across myths, dreams and lives.
  - term: Individuation
    def: Becoming more whole over a lifetime by bringing the hidden parts of ourselves into awareness, rather than fixing or cutting them off.
context: "Jung gives the labs a language for the loops that feel like they come from outside us: the person who always seems to set us off is often showing us something of our own. We pair his map with the research on attachment and emotion, and with parts work, so that what you notice in the room becomes something you can practice with."
exercise:
  title: Meet the trait that bothers you
  time: About 15 minutes, on your own or with a partner
  steps:
    - Think of someone whose behavior really gets under your skin.
    - Name the one trait that bothers you most, in a single word or phrase.
    - "Ask honestly: where does a version of that trait live in me, even a small or hidden one?"
    - Ask what that trait might have been protecting, or what it could offer if it had a healthier form.
    - Write one small way you could let that healthier form show up this week.
  why: Jung saw strong reactions to other people as clues to our own disowned parts. Turning toward the trait instead of away from it is the first step in what he called shadow work, and it often takes the charge out of the relationship that set you off.
