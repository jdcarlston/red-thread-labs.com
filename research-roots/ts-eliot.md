who:
  - Thomas Stearns Eliot was an American-born British poet whose *The Waste Land* (1922) became the defining poem of modern fragmentation.
  - "His later masterwork, *Four Quartets* (1943), turned contemplative: a meditation on time, memory and what he called “the still point of the turning world.” Written after his turn to faith, the Quartets suggest that the end of all our exploring is to return to where we began and see it as if for the first time."
concepts:
  - term: The still point
    def: A quiet center around which experience turns.
  - term: Time and memory
    def: Past and future gathered in the present moment.
  - term: Return
    def: Coming back to the familiar and seeing it new.
  - term: From fragment to whole
    def: His work moves from scattered pieces toward stillness.
context: Eliot gives the labs a poetic picture of change that doesn’t require leaving your life. Often the work is returning to the same relationships, the same body, the same history, and finally seeing them clearly.
exercise:
  title: Seeing the familiar new
  time: About 20 minutes
  steps:
    - "Choose something utterly familiar: your kitchen, a walk you take daily, a person you see every day."
    - Spend ten minutes with it as if you were seeing it for the first time.
    - Notice details you’d stopped seeing.
    - Write down three of them.
    - Ask what else in your life might look different if you saw it this way.
  why: Eliot’s Quartets are about returning and knowing a place anew. Deliberately looking at the familiar with fresh attention is a contemplative practice that loosens habit.
