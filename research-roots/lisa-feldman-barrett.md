who:
  - Lisa Feldman Barrett’s [theory of constructed emotion](https://doi.org/10.1093/scan/nsw154) holds that emotions aren’t hardwired reactions that fire the same way in everyone. The brain uses past experience and learned concepts to make sense of body sensations, and an emotion is that construction.
  - Because emotions are built from experience and concepts, they can be rebuilt. Her research on [emotional granularity](https://doi.org/10.1177/0963721414550708) suggests that people with more precise emotion concepts regulate better. Her books include *How Emotions Are Made* (2017).
concepts:
  - term: Constructed emotion
    def: The brain builds feelings from sensation, context and learned concepts.
  - term: Emotional granularity
    def: Having many precise words for feelings.
  - term: The body budget
    def: Her term for how the brain manages the body’s energy and resources.
  - term: Concepts can be learned
    def: New concepts change what you can feel.
context: "Barrett supports one of the labs’ hopeful claims: patterns of feeling, including attraction, are learned and can be relearned. Building a richer emotional vocabulary is one of the most practical ways to do that."
exercise:
  title: More than “bad”
  time: A few minutes a day, for a week
  steps:
    - Each evening, think of the strongest feeling you had that day.
    - Write the first word that comes to mind.
    - Then find two or three more precise words. Instead of “bad,” maybe “disappointed,” “left out,” “restless.”
    - Write what was happening in your body and around you at the time.
    - By the end of the week, notice whether naming feelings precisely gets easier.
  why: Barrett’s research suggests that finer-grained emotion concepts help people respond to their feelings more flexibly. Practicing precise labels each day builds that granularity.
