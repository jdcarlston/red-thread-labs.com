who:
  - "Deb Dana, a clinical social worker, turned Stephen Porges’s [polyvagal theory](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.biopsycho.2006.06.009) into a practical map in *The Polyvagal Theory in Therapy* (2018) and *Anchored* (2021). She teaches three states: safe and social, fight or flight, and shutdown, along with ways to notice which one you’re in and find a way back to connection."
  - "To be honest about the evidence: parts of the neuroscience behind polyvagal theory are [disputed by other researchers](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.biopsycho.2005.11.014). Dana’s three-state map is still widely used because it gives people plain words for what their body is doing."
concepts:
  - term: Three states
    def: Safe and social, fight or flight, and shutdown.
  - term: Neuroception
    def: Porges’s word for sensing safety or danger below awareness.
  - term: Glimmers
    def: Small moments of safety that, noticed often, build steadiness.
  - term: Co-regulation
    def: Nervous systems settling one another.
context: We use Dana’s map as a working tool, not as settled science. It gives plain language for feeling settled enough to be brave, and for checking your own state before you speak. When a conversation floods, the move is to notice you’ve left safe-and-social, take a break, and come back.
exercise:
  title: Map your three states
  time: About 20 minutes
  steps:
    - "Draw three boxes: safe and social, fight or flight, shutdown."
    - In each, write what it feels like in your body, what you tend to think, and what you tend to do.
    - Add what usually moves you from one box to another.
    - "Under safe and social, list five small glimmers that help you come back: a song, a pet, a window, a voice."
    - This week, catch one glimmer a day and pause on it for a few breaths.
  why: This is adapted from the personal state maps Dana uses in therapy. Knowing your own signs of each state makes them easier to catch early, and noticing small moments of safety on purpose trains attention toward what helps you settle.
