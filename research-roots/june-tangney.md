title: Shame, Guilt and Repair

tagline: Guilt says “I did.” Shame says “I am.”
who:
  - June Price Tangney, a psychologist at George Mason University, spent decades measuring the moral emotions. Building on Helen Block Lewis, she developed the Test of Self-Conscious Affect (TOSCA) and studied shame-proneness and guilt-proneness in children, adults, couples and people in jail.
  - "Her findings, summarized in [a 2007 review](https://doi.org/10.1146/annurev.psych.56.091103.070145), are consistent: guilt tends to lead to empathy, apology and repair, while shame tends to lead to hiding, denial, blaming others and anger. In [a long-term study of people in jail](https://doi.org/10.1177/0956797613508790), guilt-proneness predicted lower reoffending, and shame did not protect."
concepts:
  - term: Self versus behavior
    def: "Shame: “I am bad.” Guilt: “I did a bad thing.”"
  - term: Guilt leads to repair
    def: Guilt-prone people are more likely to apologize and make amends.
  - term: Shame leads to defense
    def: Shame-prone people are more likely to hide, deny or blame.
  - term: Attention
    def: Guilt keeps attention on the person harmed. Shame turns it inward.
context: "Tangney is the research ground under one of the most useful moves in the labs: shifting from “I am bad” to “I did something I want to make right.” That move turns attention outward, toward the other person, and makes repair possible. Shame is often the fuel under defensiveness and contempt in a fight."
exercise:
  title: From “I am” to “I did”
  time: About 15 minutes
  steps:
    - Think of something you did that you regret. Pick something moderate.
    - Write down the sentences you say to yourself about it. Notice any that start with “I am…” (“I’m so selfish,” “I’m a terrible friend”).
    - "Rewrite each as a behavior: “I didn’t call when I said I would.”"
    - Write how it might have affected the other person.
    - Write one concrete step toward repair, and when you’ll take it.
  why: Tangney’s research shows that focusing on the behavior, not the self, is what leads people toward empathy and repair. Rewriting the sentence is a small, practical way to make that shift.
