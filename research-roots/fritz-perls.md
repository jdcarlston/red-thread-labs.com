who:
  - Frederick “Fritz” Perls trained in psychoanalysis in Germany, fled the Nazis, and with Laura Perls and Paul Goodman developed Gestalt therapy, set out in *Gestalt Therapy* (1951). It moved therapy from talking about the past toward noticing what is happening right now, in the body, in the room, between two people.
  - Borrowing figure and ground from Gestalt psychology, he held that unmet needs stay in the foreground as unfinished business until they are completed. His demonstrations at Esalen made him famous, and Bandler and Grinder later modeled his work when developing NLP. Gestalt methods are clinical practice wisdom more than tested mechanism.
concepts:
  - term: Here and now
    def: Change comes through awareness of present experience.
  - term: Unfinished business
    def: Unexpressed feelings and unmet needs pull attention until they’re completed.
  - term: Contact and withdrawal
    def: A healthy rhythm of full contact, rest, and contact again.
  - term: Introjection
    def: Swallowing other people’s rules whole, without digesting them.
context: Perls gave the labs their attention to the present moment and to what’s happening between people in the room. Many of our practices ask “What are you aware of right now?” instead of “Why?” The thing you almost feel is often the door.
exercise:
  title: The awareness continuum
  time: About 10 minutes
  steps:
    - Sit somewhere quiet. Set a timer for five minutes.
    - "Out loud or in writing, begin each sentence with “Right now I’m aware of…” and finish it with whatever is present: a sound, a sensation, a thought, an emotion."
    - Don’t explain or judge. Just report and move to the next thing.
    - Notice when you drift into the past or future, and come back.
    - Afterward, note anything that kept returning. It may be unfinished business.
  why: This is a classic Gestalt practice. Staying with present awareness, without analysis, often brings up what needs attention, and builds the habit of noticing your experience as it happens.
