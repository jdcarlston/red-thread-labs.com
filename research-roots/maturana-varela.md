field: Biology of cognition
tagline: Everything said is said by an observer.
who:
  - Chilean biologists Humberto Maturana and Francisco Varela argued in *Autopoiesis and Cognition* (1980) and *The Tree of Knowledge* (1987) that living things are self-producing systems, and that cognition isn’t taking in a ready-made world but bringing one forth through ongoing coupling with an environment.
  - Varela, with Evan Thompson and Eleanor Rosch, carried this into *The Embodied Mind* (1991), which helped found the enactive view of cognition and brought Buddhist meditation into conversation with cognitive science.
concepts:
  - term: Autopoiesis
    def: Living systems continually produce themselves.
  - term: Structural coupling
    def: A living thing and its world shape each other over time.
  - term: Enaction
    def: We bring forth a world through how we act and perceive.
  - term: The observer
    def: Every description comes from someone standing somewhere.
context: "Maturana and Varela offer the biological version of an idea the labs return to often: we see people as we are. Change the structure doing the perceiving, slowly and in the body, and a different world shows up."
exercise:
  title: Three observers
  time: About 15 minutes
  steps:
    - Pick a person in your life you have strong opinions about.
    - Describe them in three sentences, from your point of view.
    - Now describe them as someone who loves them might.
    - Then describe them as a stranger seeing them for the first time.
    - Notice which parts of your description belong to them, and which belong to where you’re standing.
  why: Maturana’s line, that everything said is said by an observer, means every description includes the describer. Writing from several observers makes that visible.
