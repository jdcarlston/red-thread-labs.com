who:
  - "Ramana Maharshi was a South Indian sage who lived at the holy mountain Arunachala. He taught one central practice, *atma vichara* or self-inquiry: turn attention to the sense of “I” behind every thought and ask “Who am I?” until the thinking self settles into its source."
  - He taught largely through silence and short conversations, collected in *Talks with Sri Ramana Maharshi* and the short text *Who Am I?* (*Nan Yar*).
concepts:
  - term: Self-inquiry
    def: Turning attention to the one who is thinking.
  - term: The “I” thought
    def: In his teaching, the first thought, from which all others arise.
  - term: The Self
    def: Awareness itself, already whole.
  - term: Silence
    def: Teaching by presence rather than doctrine.
context: "Maharshi offers the most direct practice for the quiet center the labs point toward: the awareness underneath every story about yourself. You don’t have to share his tradition to try his question."
exercise:
  title: Who is aware?
  time: Ten minutes
  steps:
    - Sit comfortably and let your breath settle.
    - "When a thought arises, ask gently: “To whom does this thought arise?” The answer will be “to me.”"
    - "Then ask: “Who am I?” Don’t answer with words. Just turn attention toward the sense of “I.”"
    - When you drift into thinking, start again.
    - Afterward, note anything you noticed about the one who was aware.
  why: This is self-inquiry as Maharshi described it in *Who Am I?*. It is a contemplative practice, not a psychological technique, and many people find it brings a sense of spaciousness.
