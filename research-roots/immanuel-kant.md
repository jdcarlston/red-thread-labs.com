title: Persons as Ends
people: Immanuel Kant (1724–1804)
field: Moral philosophy
tagline: You are allowed to need each other. You are never allowed to use each other.
who:
  - Immanuel Kant, of Königsberg, is a hinge of modern ethics. In the *Groundwork of the Metaphysics of Morals* (1785) he argued that morality rests not on consequences or feelings but on a law reason gives itself, the categorical imperative.
  - Its most human form, the formula of humanity, says to treat people, yourself included, always as ends in themselves and never merely as means. That gives the dignity of a person a logical backbone, not just a pleasant sentiment.
concepts:
  - term: Ends, not merely means
    def: People have worth in themselves, not just as tools for our goals.
  - term: Dignity
    def: A worth that can’t be traded or replaced.
  - term: Autonomy
    def: Respecting people’s capacity to choose for themselves.
  - term: Including yourself
    def: The rule applies to how you treat yourself too.
context: Kant’s formula of humanity is the ethical ground of the labs. We can need each other, ask for things and receive help. What we don’t do is use each other. That line matters in dating, in families and in groups.
exercise:
  title: Means or end?
  time: About 15 minutes
  steps:
    - List the people you interacted with today or this week.
    - "For each, ask honestly: did I treat them only as a means to something I wanted, or also as a person with their own aims?"
    - Notice any place where you used someone, or let yourself be used.
    - Choose one interaction to repair or do differently.
    - Ask the same question about how you treated yourself.
  why: Kant’s formula of humanity is a test you can apply to any interaction. Asking it regularly builds the habit of seeing others, and yourself, as ends.
