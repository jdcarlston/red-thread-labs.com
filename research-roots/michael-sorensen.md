title: Validation

field: Communication
tagline: You don’t have to agree to make someone feel understood.
who:
  - Michael S. Sorensen is an author and communication coach, not a clinician. His book *I Hear You* (2017) defines validation as recognizing and accepting another person’s thoughts and feelings as understandable.
  - "His four steps: listen with empathy, validate the emotion, offer advice or encouragement only if it’s wanted (and ask first), then validate again. He is clear that validation is not agreement and not giving in."
concepts:
  - term: Validation
    def: Their feelings make sense. That’s different from saying they’re right.
  - term: Four steps
    def: Listen, validate, advise only if wanted, validate again.
  - term: Not agreeing
    def: You can honor the feeling without signing on to the facts.
  - term: Ask first
    def: “Do you want ideas, or do you want to vent?”
context: Sorensen makes validation simple to practice, and pairs well with Linehan’s levels. In the labs, validating first is one of the most reliable ways to lower the temperature of a conversation.
exercise:
  title: Validate before you fix
  time: Through the week
  steps:
    - The next time someone shares a problem or frustration, notice your urge to fix, reassure or explain.
    - "Instead, say something that validates the feeling: “That sounds really frustrating.” “No wonder you’re upset.”"
    - "If you want to offer advice, ask first: “Do you want ideas, or do you just want to vent?”"
    - Whatever they say, validate once more before you move on.
    - Notice how the conversation changes.
  why: Sorensen’s steps put understanding before problem-solving. People are far more open to ideas once they feel heard, and often don’t need the advice at all.
