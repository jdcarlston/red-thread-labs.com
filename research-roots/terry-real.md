who:
  - Terrence Real is a family therapist and founder of Relational Life Therapy. He wrote *I Don’t Want to Talk About It* (1997) on hidden depression in men, *The New Rules of Marriage* (2007), and *Us* (2022).
  - "He names five losing strategies in love: needing to be right, controlling your partner, unbridled self-expression, retaliation and withdrawal. His alternative is thinking relationally, asking what’s good for “us” instead of who wins, and treating each other with full respect, neither one-up nor one-down."

  - term: Full respect
    def: Neither one-up nor one-down.
  - term: Hidden depression in men

framework:
  name: The Five Losing Strategies
  intro: The moves most of us reach for when we feel threatened in a close relationship. Each one feels like protection in the moment, and each one costs closeness.
  items:
    - name: Needing to be right
      def: Arguing over whose version of events is correct. In a close relationship, winning the argument costs you the closeness.
    - name: Controlling your partner
      def: Trying to make them change, openly or through quieter pressure. Nobody likes being controlled, and it invites payback.
    - name: Unbridled self-expression
      def: Saying everything you feel the moment you feel it, with no care for how it lands. Real calls it the “barf bag” approach to intimacy.
    - name: Retaliation
      def: Hurting back because you feel hurt, openly or passive-aggressively. Real calls it offending from the victim position.
    - name: Withdrawal
      def: Pulling away to punish or because you’ve given up. A responsible time-out, where you say you’ll come back, is different.
context: "Real names, in plain terms, the moves many of us reach for when we feel threatened: fixing, correcting and convincing. The labs give people a chance to catch their own losing strategy in the moment and try something else. His work with men is especially useful for anyone taught that closeness means winning."
exercise:
  title: Your losing strategy
  time: About 15 minutes, then a week of noticing
  steps:
    - Read the five losing strategies and pick the one you use most.
    - Write down the last time you used it. What were you afraid would happen if you didn’t?
    - Write one relational question you could ask instead, like “What do we need right now?” or “What would help us here?”
    - This week, when you feel the strategy start, pause and ask the question, out loud or to yourself.
    - Note what happens when you choose closeness over winning.
  why: Real’s method starts with naming your own losing strategy without shame, then practicing a relational alternative. A ready-made question gives you something to do in the moment besides the old move.
