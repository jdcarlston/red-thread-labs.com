who:
  - Maureen Murdock is an American Jungian psychotherapist who studied with Joseph Campbell. When she asked him about a feminine version of the journey, she has written, he told her women didn’t need to make it. She disagreed.
  - "*The Heroine’s Journey* (1990) maps a different arc: turning away from the feminine, climbing into worldly achievement, a fall into an underworld, and a return that heals the split. It is a widely used companion to Campbell’s monomyth, for people of any gender."
concepts:
  - term: Separation from the feminine
    def: Leaving behind softness, intuition or the mother’s world to succeed.
  - term: The road of trials and achievement
    def: Climbing by outside standards.
  - term: The descent
    def: A fall into grief, emptiness or the underworld.
  - term: Reintegration
    def: Bringing the split-off parts back together.
context: "Murdock gives the labs language for a common story: people who succeeded by cutting off part of themselves, and who find, in midlife or after a loss, that they need it back. Descent isn’t failure. It’s part of the arc."
exercise:
  title: What you left behind
  time: About 20 minutes
  steps:
    - "Write about something you set aside in order to succeed or survive: tenderness, play, rest, creativity, a relationship."
    - Write when and why you set it down.
    - Write what it has cost.
    - Imagine welcoming it back in a small way. What would that look like this month?
    - Do one small thing that brings it back.
  why: Murdock’s journey ends in reintegrating what was split off. Naming what you left behind and inviting a small part of it back is a concrete step on that arc.
