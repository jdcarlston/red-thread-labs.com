who:
  - Bessel van der Kolk is a psychiatrist and trauma researcher. In [a 1994 paper](https://doi.org/10.3109/10673229409017088) and later in *The Body Keeps the Score* (2014), he argued that overwhelming experience is held not only as memory but as sensation, tension and a body that stays on alert long after the danger has passed.
  - "Because of that, talking alone often doesn’t reach it. He helped move trauma treatment toward the body: movement, breath, rhythm, safety and relationship. Some of his specific claims about memory are debated, but the shift toward body-based approaches has been widely influential."
concepts:
  - term: Trauma lives in the body
    def: Held as physical state and sensation, not just as a story.
  - term: Bottom-up as well as top-down
    def: Body-based work reaches what insight alone often can’t.
  - term: Safety first
    def: A settled nervous system comes before any deep processing.
  - term: Rhythm and relationship
    def: Movement, breath and being with others as ways back to steadiness.
context: "Van der Kolk supports our insistence that this work happens in the body and in a room. A pattern rooted in an old wound isn’t a bad idea to argue with. It’s a physical state to meet. That’s why the labs work bottom-up: settle first, then practice."
exercise:
  title: A rhythm reset
  time: Five minutes, once a day for a week
  steps:
    - "Choose a simple rhythm: walking, rocking, tapping your knees, or slow breathing with a longer out-breath."
    - Before you start, rate how settled you feel from 1 to 10.
    - Do the rhythm for three to five minutes. If anything feels like too much, stop and look around the room.
    - Rate yourself again and notice what changed in your body.
    - Keep a short log so you can see which rhythms help you most.
  why: Van der Kolk’s work highlights rhythm and movement as ways to bring a stressed body back to the present. Rating yourself before and after builds the habit of noticing your state, which is the first step in regulating it.
