who:
  - Martha Beck holds a PhD in sociology from Harvard and became a widely read life coach and columnist. In *Finding Your Own North Star* (2001) she distinguished the essential self, who you are underneath, from the social self, shaped to fit in.
  - "*The Way of Integrity* (2021) uses Dante’s *Inferno* as a map for moving from living out of line with your own truth to integrity: what you feel, say and do lining up. She founded the Wayfinder Life Coach Training. Her methods are coaching practice, not research."
concepts:
  - term: Essential self and social self
    def: Who you are underneath, and who you learned to be.
  - term: The body compass
    def: Your body’s response as a guide to what’s right for you.
  - term: Integrity
    def: "Being one piece: feeling, saying and doing the same thing."
  - term: The dark wood
    def: The confusion that comes from living out of line with yourself.
context: Beck offers a warm, practical way to listen to the body when making choices, which fits the labs’ attention to felt sense. Her map of integrity echoes what we mean by being yourself in relationship.
exercise:
  title: Your body compass
  time: About 15 minutes
  steps:
    - "Recall a memory that was truly awful. Notice your body for a moment: tightness, heaviness, where. Give it a score of −10."
    - Shake it off. Recall a memory that was wonderful. Notice your body. Give that +10.
    - Now think about a choice you’re facing. Imagine each option.
    - Score how your body responds to each, from −10 to +10.
    - Use the scores as information, alongside your thinking.
  why: This is Beck’s body-compass exercise. Calibrating with a clear negative and positive memory first gives you a personal scale for reading your body’s response to a choice.
