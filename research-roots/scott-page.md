who:
  - Scott Page is a professor at the University of Michigan who studies complex systems, and he has long been external faculty at the Santa Fe Institute. His lecture series *Understanding Complexity* (2009) explains how systems made of many adapting parts, like markets, cities and cultures, produce patterns none of the parts intended.
  - "*The Wisdom of Crowds* is James Surowiecki’s book. Page supplied much of the math underneath it. With Lu Hong he showed that [groups of diverse problem solvers can outperform groups of top individual performers](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.0403723101), and his diversity prediction theorem shows why a crowd’s guess can beat its average member. How far those results generalize is still debated."
concepts:
  - term: Complex, not just complicated
    def: A complicated system has many parts. A complex one has parts that adapt to each other.
  - term: Emergence
    def: Patterns that come from interactions, not from any single part.
  - term: Diversity prediction theorem
    def: A crowd’s error equals average individual error minus the diversity of their guesses.
  - term: Many-model thinking
    def: Look at a problem through several models, since each one shows something the others miss.
context: Page is a root of JD’s Wisecrowding method and of how the labs are built. A relationship is a complex system, since both people keep adapting to each other. And a room of people from many kinds of lives sees more than any one expert, which is why the labs welcome every relationship structure.
exercise:
  title: Three models
  time: About 20 minutes
  steps:
    - Pick a pattern in a relationship that puzzles you.
    - Explain it once as a feedback loop. Who does what, and how does it circle back?
    - Explain it again through attachment. What is each person trying to keep safe?
    - Explain it a third time through needs and incentives. What does each person get from the pattern?
    - Notice what each explanation shows that the others don’t, and what you’d try based on all three.
  why: Page argues that no single model is right about a complex system, and that several models used together predict and explain better than any one. Your own mind can be a small crowd.
