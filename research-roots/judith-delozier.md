tagline: Understanding isn’t something you look at. It’s somewhere you stand.
who:
  - Judith DeLozier was part of the original NLP circle at UC Santa Cruz. With John Grinder she wrote *Turtles All the Way Down* (1987), which gave the three perceptual positions their formal shape using Bateson’s idea of multiple descriptions, and co-developed NLP’s New Code.
  - "With a background in dance and anthropology, she developed somatic syntax: learning through the sequence and quality of movement. She insisted the positions be taken in the body, not just imagined. She later co-founded NLP University with Robert Dilts."
concepts:
  - term: Triple description
    def: "Self, other and observer: three ways of describing the same moment."
  - term: Standing in it
    def: Understanding as a place you physically move to.
  - term: Somatic syntax
    def: The body as the instrument of learning.
  - term: State before strategy
    def: Getting into a resourceful state first, then choosing what to do.
context: DeLozier is why the labs move people around the room. When you physically step into a different spot to take another perspective, your body changes, and so does what you can understand.
exercise:
  title: Three spots on the floor
  time: About 20 minutes
  steps:
    - "Think of a relationship you want to understand better. Mark three spots on the floor: one for you, one facing it for the other person, and one off to the side for an observer."
    - Stand in your spot. Speak or think as yourself. Notice your posture and feelings.
    - "Step out and shake it off. Then stand in their spot. Take on their posture as best you can, and speak as them: “I…”"
    - Step out, shake it off, and stand in the observer spot. Look at both. What do you notice?
    - Return to your own spot. Notice what’s different now.
  why: This is the classic perceptual-positions exercise as DeLozier taught it, with the positions taken physically. Moving your body into each place, and shaking off between them, keeps the perspectives distinct and makes them more vivid.
