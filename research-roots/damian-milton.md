title: The Double Empathy Problem
people: Damian Milton
field: Sociology · Autism studies
tagline: Misunderstanding runs both ways.
who:
  - Damian Milton is a British sociologist and autistic scholar. His [2012 paper](https://doi.org/10.1080/09687599.2012.710008) named the double empathy problem. The standard view blamed autistic people for failing at social understanding. Milton pointed out that non-autistic people misread autistic people just as much, and that breakdowns happen between people who experience the world differently, not inside one of them.
  - Research has started to back this up. [A 2020 study](https://doi.org/10.1177/1362361320919286) found that information passed between autistic people as well as between non-autistic people, and broke down mainly in mixed pairs. Other work has linked [masking](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10803-017-3166-5), hiding one’s natural ways of being to fit in, to exhaustion. The field is young, so “research suggests” is the honest phrasing.
concepts:
  - term: Double empathy
    def: When people experience the world very differently, misunderstanding goes both ways.
  - term: Mismatch, not deficit
    def: The breakdown lives in the interaction, not in one person.
  - term: Masking
    def: Suppressing natural ways of being to pass, often at a real cost.
  - term: Interactional expertise
    def: People who are often misread tend to become expert at reading others, and rarely get the same in return.
context: Milton reframes a pain many people carry, of being the odd one out, from “something is wrong with me” to “we weren’t reaching each other.” That matters well beyond autism. The work isn’t learning to pass. It’s finding the rooms and people where you’re met, and accepting that some rooms won’t adjust.
exercise:
  title: Translate both ways
  time: About 15 minutes
  steps:
    - Think of someone you often misunderstand, or who often misunderstands you.
    - "Write down how you communicate: pace, directness, how much detail, how you show care."
    - Write down how they seem to communicate, without judging it as right or wrong.
    - Find one place where your styles collide.
    - Ask them one question about how they like to talk or be checked in with, and share one thing about how you do.
  why: The double empathy framework suggests misunderstanding comes from a mismatch between people. Describing both styles side by side moves the problem from inside one person to the space between, where both people can adjust.
