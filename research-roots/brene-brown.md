tagline: Vulnerability is where connection starts.
who:
  - Brené Brown, a research professor of social work at the University of Houston, studied shame, vulnerability and what she calls wholeheartedness through thousands of interviews. Her [shame resilience theory](https://doi.org/10.1606/1044-3894.3483) describes how people recognize shame and move through it with empathy.
  - "She popularized the distinction between shame (“I am bad”) and guilt (“I did something bad”), building on June Tangney’s experimental work, and found that people with a strong sense of connection shared the courage to be imperfect and seen. Her findings are qualitative: research-informed language for what people feel, more than a tested method of change."
concepts:
  - term: Shame resilience
    def: Shame grows in secrecy and judgment and shrinks when spoken and met with empathy.
  - term: Vulnerability
    def: The courage to be seen without guarantees.
  - term: Empathy and sympathy
    def: Feeling with someone, not looking down from outside.
  - term: “The story I’m telling myself”
    def: Naming the quick first-draft story so it can be checked.
  - term: Belonging and fitting in
    def: Fitting in changes you to be accepted. Belonging asks you to be who you are.
context: "Brown names the fear at the center of many patterns: that if people really saw us, they’d leave. The labs give people safe practice at being seen, so it gets more survivable each time."
exercise:
  title: The story I’m telling myself
  time: About 10 minutes
  steps:
    - "Think of a recent moment that stung: a short reply, being left out, a look."
    - "Finish this sentence honestly, even if it sounds dramatic: “The story I’m telling myself is…”"
    - List what you actually know, and what you’re guessing.
    - Write one other possible story.
    - "If it feels safe, try saying it to the person: “The story I’m telling myself is… Can you help me understand?”"
  why: Brown uses this phrase to catch the fast stories our minds write under stress. Saying it out loud owns the story as yours and invites the other person to share theirs, instead of acting on a guess.
