tagline: We are built to belong.
who:
  - Julianne Holt-Lunstad’s [2010 meta-analysis](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pmed.1000316) pooled 148 studies and found that people with stronger social relationships had about a 50% greater likelihood of survival over the study periods. [A 2015 follow-up](https://doi.org/10.1177/1745691614568352) found loneliness and isolation raised mortality risk on a scale comparable to well-known health risks.
  - In 2023, U.S. Surgeon General Vivek Murthy issued an advisory, *Our Epidemic of Loneliness and Isolation*, naming social connection a public-health priority.
concepts:
  - term: Connection and survival
    def: Strong ties predict longer, healthier lives.
  - term: Loneliness as a health risk
    def: Comparable in size to other major risk factors.
  - term: A public-health issue
    def: Named as a national priority in 2023.
  - term: Quality and quantity
    def: Both how many ties you have and how good they are matter.
context: This research sets the stakes for everything the labs do. Belonging isn’t a luxury. Practicing connection skills, in real rooms with real people, is a health practice.
exercise:
  title: A week of reaching out
  time: Five minutes a day, for a week
  steps:
    - Make a quick list of ten people you’re glad to know, including some you’ve lost touch with.
    - "Each day, reach out to one: a text, a voice note, a call, an invitation."
    - "Make it specific: mention something you remember or appreciate about them."
    - Notice how you feel before and after, and how they respond.
    - At the end of the week, pick two people to see in person in the next month.
  why: Holt-Lunstad’s work shows that social ties affect health, and small, regular contact is how ties are kept alive. The Surgeon General’s 2023 advisory makes the same point for individuals, encouraging people to reach out to a friend or family member for about 15 minutes each day. A daily reach-out turns a vague wish to connect into a habit.
