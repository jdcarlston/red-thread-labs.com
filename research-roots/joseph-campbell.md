title: The Hero’s Journey
people: Joseph Campbell (1904–1987)
field: Comparative mythology
tagline: The one story humanity keeps telling about how a person becomes.
who:
  - "Joseph Campbell was an American scholar of comparative mythology. In *The Hero with a Thousand Faces* (1949) he proposed the monomyth: a shared pattern beneath the world’s hero stories, with a call, a threshold, an ordeal, a reward and a return."
  - His conversations with Bill Moyers, published as *The Power of Myth* (1988), brought the idea into popular culture, along with his phrase “follow your bliss.” His map shapes much of modern storytelling. Scholars have debated how universal the pattern really is.
concepts:
  - term: The call
    def: Something disrupts ordinary life and asks for change.
  - term: The threshold
    def: Crossing from the known into the unknown.
  - term: The ordeal
    def: The hardest test, where the old self is challenged.
  - term: The return
    def: Coming home changed, with something to offer.
context: Campbell’s map helps people see their own hard seasons as part of a shape, not a dead end. The labs use it alongside Maureen Murdock’s heroine’s journey, which adds the descent his version left out.
exercise:
  title: Map a turning point
  time: About 25 minutes
  steps:
    - Choose a major change in your life that is now behind you.
    - Write what your ordinary world was before it.
    - "Write the call: what disrupted things?"
    - Write the threshold, the ordeal, and what you gained.
    - "Write the return: what did you bring back to others? Notice what the shape tells you about a change you’re in now."
  why: Narrative research suggests that people who can tell their hardships as stories of growth tend to cope better. Mapping a past change onto Campbell’s stages is a simple way to practice that kind of storytelling.
