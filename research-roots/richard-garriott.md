who:
  - "Richard Garriott created the Ultima series of computer role-playing games. *Ultima IV: Quest of the Avatar* (1985) is widely credited as one of the first games to make moral growth, not defeating a villain, the way to win."
  - "Players had to live out eight virtues, Honesty, Compassion, Valor, Justice, Sacrifice, Honor, Spirituality and Humility, built from three principles: Truth, Love and Courage. Garriott has said those three principles echo the Scarecrow, Tin Woodman and Lion of Oz. He tells the story in his memoir *Explore/Create* (2017)."
concepts:
  - term: The Avatar
    def: The player becomes a model of virtue, not just a fighter.
  - term: Eight virtues
    def: Honesty, compassion, valor, justice, sacrifice, honor, spirituality, humility.
  - term: Three principles
    def: Truth, love and courage.
  - term: Consequences
    def: The game remembers how you treat people.
context: Garriott is in the lineage because he took a playful form seriously as moral practice, which is what the labs try to do. Chick Adams, JD’s partner and co-founder, knows his work well.
exercise:
  title: A virtue a day
  time: A few minutes a day, for eight days
  steps:
    - Write the eight virtues on a card.
    - Each morning, pick one for the day.
    - Look for one moment to practice it deliberately, as if the game were watching.
    - Each night, note what you did and how it felt.
    - After eight days, notice which virtues come easily and which you’d like to grow.
  why: Ultima IV made virtue something you practice through repeated choices. Treating a virtue as the day’s quest borrows that structure for real life.
