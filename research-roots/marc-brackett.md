who:
  - "Marc Brackett directs the Yale Center for Emotional Intelligence and created RULER: Recognizing, Understanding, Labeling, Expressing and Regulating emotion. It is a research-based approach now used in many schools, and [a classroom trial](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.lindif.2011.10.002) linked it to better grades and social skills."
  - His book *Permission to Feel* (2019) argues that emotion skills can be learned, and that giving ourselves and others permission to feel is where well-being starts. One of his best-known tools is the Mood Meter, which sorts feelings by energy and pleasantness.
concepts:
  - term: RULER
    def: "Recognize, understand, label, express, regulate: emotion as learnable skills."
  - term: Permission to feel
    def: Allowing emotion, rather than suppressing it, is where regulation begins.
  - term: Emotions as information
    def: Feelings carry information about what we need.
  - term: The Mood Meter
    def: A simple grid of energy and pleasantness that builds precise emotion words.
context: Brackett is part of the evidence that emotional skill can be taught, which is the whole premise of a lab. His careful, empirical approach is the standard we aim for.
exercise:
  title: The Mood Meter
  time: One minute, three times a day, for a week
  steps:
    - "Draw a square split into four: high energy and unpleasant (top left), high energy and pleasant (top right), low energy and unpleasant (bottom left), low energy and pleasant (bottom right)."
    - Three times a day, place yourself on the grid.
    - "Then find the most precise word for the feeling: not just “bad,” but “anxious,” “disappointed,” “drained.”"
    - "Ask: what caused this, and what do I need?"
    - At the end of the week, look at where you spent most of your time.
  why: The Mood Meter is a core RULER tool. Brackett’s research suggests that recognizing and precisely labeling feelings is the foundation for regulating them, and the grid makes that quick to practice.
