tagline: The shame was never the desire. It was being alone with it.
who:
  - Nancy Friday was a writer who collected people’s sexual fantasies in their own words and published them. *My Secret Garden* (1973) gathered women’s fantasies at a time when female desire was barely discussed in public. *Men in Love* (1980) and *Women on Top* (1991) followed.
  - "Her method was journalism, not laboratory science: letters, interviews and first-person accounts. But the effect was a landmark. It showed that desire is far wider and more common than the shame around it suggested."
concepts:
  - term: The inner erotic life
    def: A private landscape most people hide and few understand.
  - term: Removing the shame
    def: Hearing the range of what others feel dissolves the sense of “only me.”
  - term: Women’s desire, spoken
    def: She gave voice to desire when the culture denied women had it.
  - term: The gap
    def: The distance between how we present and what we privately want.
context: Friday is part of why the labs treat desire without shame. Knowing how wide the range of human desire is helps people stop treating their inner life as a secret flaw. What anyone does with that inner life stays their own choice.
exercise:
  title: Only me?
  time: About 10 minutes, private
  steps:
    - Write down one wish or desire, of any kind, that you’ve kept to yourself because you worried it was strange.
    - Notice what you feel as you write it.
    - "Ask: who taught me this was something to hide?"
    - Consider that many people likely carry something similar.
    - Decide whether you want to share it with anyone. Keeping it private is a fine choice too.
  why: Friday’s collections showed that private desires are far more common than people assume, and that isolation feeds shame. Naming a hidden wish to yourself, and questioning where the shame came from, can loosen its hold.
