who:
  - "[Pearson and colleagues](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0954579400004636) named *earned security*: adults who describe a genuinely difficult childhood yet make sense of it coherently, and who parent as warmly as people who had good-enough beginnings, though the old pain can still weigh on their mood."
  - "[Roisman and colleagues](https://doi.org/10.1111/1467-8624.00467) tested the idea against a study that followed people from birth into their twenties, and refined it. Coherent security can be reached despite adversity."
concepts:
  - term: Earned security
    def: A coherent, secure stance reached despite a painful history.
  - term: Coherence over history
    def: What predicts adult security is how you make sense of your story, not how good it was.
  - term: The Adult Attachment Interview
    def: "[Mary Main’s method](https://doi.org/10.2307/3333827), in which security shows up as a coherent story told under pressure."
  - term: Reorganization is real
    def: Attachment patterns can change across a life.
context: "This is the research behind “the map can change.” The patterns that feel like *who you are* can be re-mapped and re-trained. To be honest about the evidence: it supports reaching coherent security, but the simple “insecure as a baby, secure as an adult” story is still debated and the measures are imperfect. The labs stay on the ground the research supports: security can be *earned*."
exercise:
  title: Five words, five memories
  time: About 30 minutes, somewhere private
  steps:
    - Choose five words that describe your relationship with one caregiver when you were young.
    - For each word, write down one specific memory that shows it, an actual moment rather than a summary.
    - Notice where a memory backs up its word, and where it doesn’t quite fit. Both are useful.
    - Write two or three sentences about how that relationship shaped you, and what you’d like to keep or change.
    - Read it all back. Does the story hang together? Does it hold both what was hard and what was good?
  why: This is adapted from the [Adult Attachment Interview](https://doi.org/10.2307/3333827), which asks people to describe a caregiver relationship in five words and then back each word with memories. The interview judges security by how coherently the story is told, not by how good the childhood was. That is why earned security is possible. This version is for reflection only. The real interview is given and scored by trained researchers.
