who:
  - Donella “Dana” Meadows (1941–2001) was a scientist, teacher, farmer and writer. She was lead author of *The Limits to Growth* (1972), the computer-modeling study for the Club of Rome that showed how growth on a finite planet runs into limits. She taught at Dartmouth, wrote a weekly newspaper column for years, and founded the Sustainability Institute in Vermont.
  - Her short essay [*Leverage Points*](https://donellameadows.org/archives/leverage-points-places-to-intervene-in-a-system/) ranks twelve places to intervene in a system, from changing numbers (weakest) to changing the mindset the system grows out of (strongest). Her primer *Thinking in Systems* was published after her death and is still one of the clearest introductions to the field.
concepts:
  - term: Stocks and flows
    def: What builds up over time, and what fills it or drains it.
  - term: Feedback loops
    def: Balancing loops hold things steady. Reinforcing loops make them grow or collapse.
  - term: Leverage points
    def: Places where a small shift changes the whole system.
  - term: Paradigm
    def: The shared beliefs a system grows from. The deepest place to change it.
context: Meadows wrote about economies and ecosystems, but her ideas fit relationships. Trust is a stock that builds and drains. Arguments run on loops. And like her, the labs work at the deepest leverage point we can reach, which is the belief underneath the pattern.
exercise:
  title: Find the leverage
  time: About 20 minutes
  steps:
    - Pick a pattern you keep trying to change, in yourself or with someone else.
    - List what you’ve already tried. Notice how much of it is more effort, more rules, or more of the same.
    - Ask what this pattern is really serving. What goal is it quietly meeting?
    - Ask what belief it grows from. Write it as one sentence.
    - Choose one small change at the level of the goal or the belief, and try it for a week.
  why: In Meadows’ ranking, adjusting numbers and effort is the weakest place to intervene, and changing goals and mindsets is the strongest. Most of us start at the bottom of her list. Starting near the top saves a lot of work.
