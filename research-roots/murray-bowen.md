who:
  - "Murray Bowen, an American psychiatrist who worked at the Menninger Clinic, the National Institute of Mental Health and Georgetown, developed family systems theory: a family works as one emotional unit, and a person’s struggles make sense only inside it."
  - His eight concepts include differentiation of self, triangles, emotional cutoff and the passing of patterns down the generations. Differentiation, staying emotionally connected while thinking and acting for yourself, is the center of it all. David Schnarch carried it into couple work and Harriet Lerner into popular writing.
concepts:
  - term: Differentiation of self
    def: Holding your own position while staying in contact.
  - term: Triangles
    def: Tension between two people gets pulled onto a third.
  - term: Emotional cutoff
    def: Managing an unresolved bond through distance, which keeps it unresolved.
  - term: Multigenerational transmission
    def: Patterns pass down the family line.
context: "Bowen explains why a family gathering can pull you back into an old role the moment you walk in. He also offers the way through: you can change the dance by changing your own steps, without cutting off and without giving yourself up. JD’s consulting work draws on Bowen’s ideas."
exercise:
  title: A three-generation map
  time: About 40 minutes
  steps:
    - Draw a simple family tree going back to your grandparents. Squares or circles and lines are enough.
    - "Next to each person, write a word or two about their role: the peacemaker, the rebel, the caretaker, the one who left."
    - Mark close bonds, conflicts, and cutoffs with different kinds of lines.
    - Look for patterns that repeat across generations.
    - Write one sentence about a pattern you’ve inherited, and one about how you’d like to hold it differently.
  why: This is a simple version of the genogram, the family diagram used in Bowen-based therapy. Seeing patterns laid out across generations makes them feel less personal and more like a system, which is the first step toward differentiating from them.
