who:
  - John O’Donohue was an Irish poet and philosopher, born in Connemara and raised in the Burren in County Clare. He was a Catholic priest for many years, earned a doctorate in philosophical theology at Tübingen, and later left the priesthood to write and teach.
  - "His book *Anam Ċara: A Book of Celtic Wisdom* (1997) became an international bestseller. In the early Celtic church, an anam cara, a soul friend, was someone to whom you could reveal the hidden parts of your life without a mask."

context: "O’Donohue names what many people come to the labs looking for: someone with whom they can drop the mask. His idea of the soul friend is the poetic side of what attachment research calls a secure base."
exercise:
  title: Your soul friends
  time: About 20 minutes
  steps:
    - Write the names of people with whom you’ve felt fully known, at any time in your life.
    - Next to each, write what made it possible.
    - If the list is short or empty, write what you would want in such a friendship.
    - Choose one person, on the list or not, with whom you could share one more true thing.
    - Share it this week, and notice what happens.
  why: O’Donohue described the soul friend as someone you can reveal yourself to without pretense. Naming what made those friendships possible helps you look for, and offer, the same.
