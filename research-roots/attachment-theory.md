tagline: We are shaped by who was there when we reached out.
who:
  - Bowlby showed that the bond between child and caregiver isn’t learned dependency. It’s an evolved survival system, as basic as feeding. Ainsworth’s [“Strange Situation” studies](https://doi.org/10.2307/1127388) identified secure, anxious-ambivalent and avoidant patterns in how infants use a caregiver as a safe base and a haven. Mary Main and Judith Solomon later added the [disorganized pattern](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5817243/).
  - Their work is the backbone of adult attachment research, Emotionally Focused Therapy, and our whole picture of how early safety, or its absence, organizes a life.
concepts:
  - term: Safe base & haven
    def: A secure figure is both a base to explore from and a haven to return to. Feeling safe is what lets a person venture out.
  - term: The four patterns
    def: "**Secure**, **anxious-ambivalent**, **avoidant** and **disorganized** (added later by Mary Main): strategies for staying connected to an imperfect caregiver."
  - term: Internal working models
    def: Early bonds lay down quiet expectations about the self (“am I worth care?”) and others (“will they come?”) that carry into adulthood.
  - term: The Strange Situation
    def: "[Ainsworth’s observational method](https://doi.org/10.2307/1127388): a short separation and reunion reveal the strategy a child uses under stress."
context: The urge to *reach toward* and the urge to *withdraw from* that people bring into a lab are these patterns, grown up. Early expectations of “am I worth care?” and “will they come?” run quietly underneath. The work doesn’t relabel anyone. It helps people build an *earned* security where a given one was missing.
exercise:
  title: Map your safe base and haven
  time: About 15 minutes, with a pen
  steps:
    - Write down one to three people, places or practices you go back to when you’re shaken. These are your *haven*.
    - Write down what makes it feel safe to try something new or take a risk. That’s your *base*. It may be the same list, or not.
    - Think of one stressful moment from the last week. In one line, without judging it, write whether you reached toward someone or pulled away.
    - "Choose one small reach for the coming week: a text, a call or a walk with someone from your haven list. Afterward, notice how it felt."
  why: "[Ainsworth found](https://doi.org/10.4324/9780203758045) that a secure figure does two jobs: a base to explore from and a haven to come back to. Under stress, people lean on a habitual strategy of reaching or withdrawing. Seeing your own strategy, and practicing a small reach, is the first step toward choosing it."
