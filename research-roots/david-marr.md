title: Three Levels of Analysis
people: David Marr (1945–1980)
field: Computational neuroscience · Vision
tagline: Mind and brain aren’t rivals. They’re one system, described at different levels.
who:
  - "David Marr, in *Vision* (1982), argued that any information-processing system, a mind or a machine, has to be understood at three levels: what problem it solves and why (computational), how it solves it (algorithmic), and what physically carries it out (implementation)."
  - A fact at one level doesn’t reduce to a fact at another. His framework became a foundation of cognitive science and a clear way out of “mind versus brain” arguments.
concepts:
  - term: Computational level
    def: What is being solved, and why.
  - term: Algorithmic level
    def: The steps and representations used.
  - term: Implementation level
    def: The physical stuff, neurons or circuits, that runs it.
  - term: Levels are not rivals
    def: Each is a true description. None is the only real one.
context: "Marr gives the labs a precise way to say that patterns live at several levels at once: what they’re for, how they run, and how the body carries them. Change at any level helps. Change at all three lasts."
exercise:
  title: A habit at three levels
  time: About 15 minutes
  steps:
    - Pick a habit you want to change.
    - "Computational: What problem is it solving? What is it for?"
    - "Algorithmic: What are the steps? What triggers it, what happens next, what ends it?"
    - "Implementation: What in your body and setting supports it? Sleep, hunger, time of day, your phone, a room."
    - Choose one small change at each level.
  why: Marr’s framework says a system is only understood when all three levels are described. Applying it to a habit gives you three different places to intervene.
