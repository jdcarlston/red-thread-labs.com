tagline: Notice what you want more of, and mark it the moment it happens.
who:
  - "B. F. Skinner’s research on operant conditioning showed that behavior is shaped by its consequences: actions followed by something rewarding become more likely. He also showed how complex behavior can be built step by step, by reinforcing small approximations of it, a process he called shaping."
  - "Karen Pryor, a marine-mammal trainer and biologist, brought that science into gentle, humane use. Her book *Don’t Shoot the Dog!* (1984) applies positive reinforcement to animals, children, partners and ourselves, and she popularized clicker training, which uses a distinct signal to mark the exact moment of a wanted behavior."
concepts:
  - term: Positive reinforcement
    def: Following a behavior with something the other values makes it more likely to happen again.
  - term: The marker
    def: A clear, quick signal, a click or a warm “yes,” that tells someone exactly which moment earned the reward.
  - term: Shaping
    def: Building toward a bigger change by rewarding small steps in the right direction, rather than waiting for the finished behavior.
  - term: Catch them getting it right
    def: Attention is a reward. Noticing what goes well works better, and feels better, than correcting what goes wrong.
context: "Pryor gives the labs a kind, precise way to think about habit and change, in ourselves and in the people we love. Most of us try to change each other by pointing out what’s wrong. Shaping turns that around: you notice the small moments you want more of and mark them warmly, right away. It’s the same principle behind Gottman’s research on appreciation, made practical."
exercise:
  title: The training game
  time: About 20 minutes, in a group of three or more
  steps:
    - One person, the learner, steps out of the room. The others agree on a simple action for the learner to do, like touching a lamp or sitting in a certain chair.
    - One person is the trainer. When the learner comes back, the trainer says “yes!” (or clicks) whenever the learner moves even slightly toward the goal. No words, hints or corrections.
    - The learner keeps moving and experimenting, following the yeses, until they find the action.
    - Switch roles and play a few rounds.
    - "Afterward, talk about it: what was it like to be guided only by yeses? Then choose one small thing you’d like more of from someone in your life, and practice noticing and thanking them the moment it happens this week."
  why: This is the training game Pryor describes in *Don’t Shoot the Dog!*, used to teach shaping to people. Feeling how quickly someone learns from well-timed yeses alone, with no criticism at all, makes the case for positive reinforcement better than any explanation.
