tagline: People change not when they’re pushed, but when they’re prized.
who:
  - "Carl Rogers, a founder of humanistic psychology, held that every person carries a built-in drive toward growth, which he called the actualizing tendency. In [a 1957 paper](https://doi.org/10.1037/h0045357) he named the conditions that let it flourish: unconditional positive regard, accurate empathy, and genuineness (congruence)."
  - He moved therapy away from an expert fixing a patient and toward a relationship of deep acceptance, trusting the client to lead their own change. *On Becoming a Person* (1961) carried the ideas far beyond therapy.
concepts:
  - term: Unconditional positive regard
    def: Valuing a person without conditions of worth.
  - term: The actualizing tendency
    def: A built-in drive toward growth, given the right conditions.
  - term: Empathy and congruence
    def: Accurate understanding and genuineness as the conditions for change.
  - term: Conditions of worth
    def: The learned “I’m only lovable if…” that distorts how we see ourselves.
context: "Rogers is the humanistic root of how the labs treat people: as whole and fundamentally good, not as problems to fix. His famous line, that accepting yourself as you are is what makes change possible, describes the paradox at the center of the work."
exercise:
  title: Ten minutes of being heard
  time: About 25 minutes, with a partner
  steps:
    - Sit with a friend or partner. One of you talks for ten minutes about something that matters to you. The other only listens.
    - "The listener doesn’t advise, fix, or share their own story. Every so often, they reflect back what they hear: “It sounds like you felt…”"
    - The speaker can correct the reflection until it fits.
    - Switch roles.
    - Afterward, each of you share what it was like to be listened to that way.
  why: Rogers found that people grow when they feel accurately understood and accepted. Reflective listening without advice is the simplest form of that, and most people rarely get it.
