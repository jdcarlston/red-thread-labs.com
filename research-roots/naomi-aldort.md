who:
  - Naomi Aldort is the author of *Raising Our Children, Raising Ourselves* (2005). Her core contribution is SALVE, a five-step way to interrupt a parent’s automatic reaction and replace control with attention, listening, validation and trust.
  - She reframes “misbehavior” as an unmet need and treats unconditional love as the setting a child grows in, not a reward for good behavior. Her work is parenting education rather than research, and the steps apply well beyond parenting.
concepts:
  - term: "S: Separate"
    def: Separate yourself from the other person’s behavior. Stop the movie of your own reaction.
  - term: "A: Attention"
    def: Turn your full attention to them and to what is actually happening.
  - term: "L: Listen"
    def: Listen to their words, or their actions if they don’t have words.
  - term: "V: Validate"
    def: Acknowledge the feeling, without drama or criticism.
  - term: "E: Empower"
    def: Step back and trust them to work it through.
context: "Aldort’s SALVE puts self-regulation first: your reaction, not their behavior, is the first thing to work with. That’s true with children, partners and colleagues alike. It fits the labs’ emphasis on settling yourself before you respond."
exercise:
  title: SALVE in one moment
  time: Through the week
  steps:
    - The next time someone’s behavior sets you off, child or adult, pause before you respond.
    - "Separate: notice the story you’re telling yourself about them, and set it aside for a moment."
    - "Attention and Listen: look at them and take in what they’re saying or doing, without planning your reply."
    - "Validate: name their feeling simply: “You’re really disappointed.”"
    - "Empower: ask “What do you need?” or wait, instead of fixing. Notice what happens."
  why: SALVE interrupts the automatic reaction that usually escalates conflict. The first step, separating from your own story, is what makes the rest possible.
