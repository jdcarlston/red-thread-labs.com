concepts:
  - term: A wheel, not a ladder
    def: Enter at any letter. Every pillar is a valid place to start.
  - term: Self to other
    def: The movement runs from settling in yourself to reaching toward another, and the two H’s mark each end.
  - term: Descent and return
    def: Inhabit is the floor you go down to. Savor and See are the rise back up.
  - term: Rotate, Interrupt, Re-weave
    def: The engine at the center, Pair-Bond Refactoring. Notice the loop, break it as it starts, and weave in something new.
framework:
  name: The seven pillars
  intro: Seven letters seat all nine rotations. Two pillars each hold a pair.
  items:
    - name: Center · Brave
      mark: C
      def: Settle the body until it’s ready, not just calm. Ready enough to risk contact.
    - name: Home · Being
      mark: H
      def: Come home to the self underneath the inherited twist, the ground that was here before the story.
    - name: Enter · Free and At
      mark: E
      def: Grow fluid enough to move, so you can step inside another person’s world. Empathy from the inside.
    - name: Release · Peace
      mark: R
      def: Let go of the two demands. Forgiveness as a release, not a decision you force.
    - name: Inhabit · Within
      mark: I
      def: Go all the way inside a position instead of performing it from outside. The depth move.
    - name: Savor and See · Joyfully and Cherishing
      mark: S
      def: Rise into gratitude before it has an object, and into the witness that holds both inner worlds.
    - name: Honor · Love
      mark: H
      def: Extend yourself for the spiritual growth of another. One letter from cherished.
context: "The CHERISH Wheel is how the labs hold the whole curriculum in one picture. People find themselves on it, see where they tend to get stuck, and see that the way to being cherished runs through cherishing, themselves first and then someone else. It also gives facilitators a shared map for where a lab sits. It rests on the definition of love from [M. Scott Peck](/library/m-scott-peck/), on the [Destination Method](/library/robert-dee-mcdonald/), and on [memory reconsolidation](/library/memory-reconsolidation/), the research showing that a recalled pattern can be updated before it’s stored again ([Nader, Schafe & LeDoux, 2000](https://doi.org/10.1038/35021052)). The wheel itself is a curriculum map from practice, not a tested model."
exercise:
  title: Find your letter
  time: About 15 minutes
  steps:
    - Read the seven pillars slowly.
    - Pick the letter you live in most easily, and the one you avoid.
    - Write one recent moment from each, what you did and how it felt in your body.
    - "For the letter you avoid, write one small move you could make this week: a moment of Center, a step toward Home, a first Release."
    - At the end of the week, notice whether the letter you avoid feels any closer.
  why: The wheel works because any letter is a valid entry. Starting from where you already are, and taking one small step toward the letter you avoid, is the same rotate-interrupt-re-weave move the wheel describes, practiced on a scale you can manage.
