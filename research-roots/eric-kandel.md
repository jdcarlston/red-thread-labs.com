tagline: Practice is not a metaphor. It grows new connections.
who:
  - Eric Kandel studied the sea slug *Aplysia*, whose large neurons can be counted and watched, to show how learning works at the level of the synapse. Short-term memory changes the strength of existing connections. Long-term memory switches on genes that build new ones. He shared the 2000 Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine for this work.
  - "His [2001 review](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1067020) and his memoir *In Search of Memory* (2006) describe the cellular proof that experience physically rewrites the brain. One finding stands out for practice: repeated training spaced over time produces longer-lasting memory than the same training crammed together."
concepts:
  - term: Synaptic plasticity
    def: Learning changes how strong and how many connections are.
  - term: Short-term and long-term
    def: One tunes existing connections. The other builds new ones.
  - term: Spaced training
    def: Practice spread over time lasts longer.
  - term: Experience is physical
    def: Practice doesn’t just feel different. It changes tissue.
context: "Kandel is the deepest floor under our emphasis on reps: practice with real feeling grows new connections. It’s also why the labs are built as a series with time in between, rather than one big weekend."
exercise:
  title: Spaced practice
  time: Five minutes on four days spread over two weeks
  steps:
    - Choose one skill from a lab or from your own life, such as naming a feeling or making a clear request.
    - "Schedule four short practice sessions: day 1, day 3, day 7 and day 14."
    - In each session, practice the skill a few times out loud or with a friend.
    - Between sessions, leave it alone.
    - After day 14, notice how easily the skill comes compared to day 1.
  why: Kandel’s work showed that spaced repetitions create lasting memory at the cellular level, a pattern also seen in human learning research. Spreading practice out is a simple way to help new skills stick.
