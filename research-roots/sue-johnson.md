who:
  - "Sue Johnson was a clinical psychologist in Ottawa who, with Leslie Greenberg in the 1980s, developed Emotionally Focused Therapy (EFT) for couples. She took Bowlby’s attachment theory into adult love and read most couple fights as protest: underneath the criticism or the stonewalling, each partner is asking “Are you there for me? Do I matter to you?”"
  - EFT became one of the best-studied couple therapies. [A 1999 review of the outcome studies](https://doi.org/10.1093/clipsy.6.1.67) found large effects, and [a 2013 brain-imaging study](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0079314) found that after EFT, holding a partner’s hand calmed the brain’s response to threat. Her book *Hold Me Tight* (2008) brought the work to couples directly.
concepts:
  - term: The negative cycle
    def: Loops like pursue and withdraw that trap a couple. The cycle, not the partner, is the problem.
  - term: Attachment panic
    def: Anger and distance as a protest when the bond suddenly feels threatened.
  - term: A.R.E.
    def: "Accessible, Responsive, Engaged: the three questions a secure bond answers “yes.”"
  - term: Demon dialogues
    def: "Her names for the three common cycles: Find the Bad Guy, the Protest Polka, and Freeze and Flee."
context: "Johnson is the bridge from Bowlby’s nursery to the grown-up relationship, and the clinical case that co-regulation is real and can be practiced. The labs borrow her core move: get underneath the strategy to the longing it protects. When a pattern repeats between two people, we treat the loop as the thing to study, so neither person has to be the villain."
exercise:
  title: Name the cycle, not the culprit
  time: About 20 minutes, alone first, then with your partner if you choose
  steps:
    - Think of a recent argument that felt familiar, the kind you’ve had before.
    - Write down your move (what you did or said) and the other person’s move. Keep it to behaviors, not judgments.
    - "Under your move, write what you were feeling underneath it: scared, alone, not good enough, not wanted. Do the same for your guess about them."
    - Give the loop a name, something like “the chase” or “the shutdown.”
    - "If it feels safe, share the name and say: “I think this is what gets us. I don’t want it to win.”"
  why: This follows the first conversation in *Hold Me Tight*, recognizing the demon dialogues. EFT’s research shows couples change when they see the cycle as the enemy and start sharing the softer feelings underneath it. Naming the loop together turns two opponents into two people facing the same problem.
