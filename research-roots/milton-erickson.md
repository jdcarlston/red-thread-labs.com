tagline: Nothing you bring into the room is the wrong thing. It is the material.
who:
  - Milton Erickson, an American psychiatrist, reshaped clinical hypnosis into a conversational, permissive art. Paralyzed by polio as a teenager, he rebuilt movement partly by recalling his body’s own memory of moving, an early lesson in the resources already inside a person.
  - His therapy was brief, indirect and tailored. He used whatever a client brought, their language, beliefs and even resistance, as material for change. Jay Haley’s *Uncommon Therapy* (1973) made him widely known, and Bandler and Grinder modeled his language patterns as one of NLP’s main sources.
concepts:
  - term: Utilization
    def: Whatever a person brings, symptom, story or resistance, is something to work with.
  - term: Pacing and leading
    def: Meet someone’s present experience first, then invite a next step.
  - term: Indirect suggestion
    def: Permissive language that lets the listener find their own meaning.
  - term: Inner resources
    def: People already hold more resources than they know how to reach.
context: "Erickson’s utilization is a quiet principle in the labs: nothing a person brings is the wrong thing. Resistance is information, and the resources for change are usually already there, waiting to be found."
exercise:
  title: Find the resource you already have
  time: About 15 minutes
  steps:
    - "Name a quality you wish you had more of in a hard situation: calm, confidence, patience, playfulness."
    - "Recall a time, in any part of your life, when you had even a little of it. It can be small: calm with a pet, confident at a hobby."
    - Go back into that memory. Notice how you were standing, breathing, speaking.
    - "Find one small physical cue from it: a posture, a breath, a phrase."
    - Next time the hard situation comes, use the cue and see what comes with it.
  why: Erickson often helped people reach capacities they already had in other contexts. Recalling a real example in detail, and linking it to a simple cue, is a common way of drawing on that resource where you need it.
