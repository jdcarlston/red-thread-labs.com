tagline: You are already a scientist. The question is whether you’re still testing.
who:
  - "George Kelly built personal construct theory in *The Psychology of Personal Constructs* (1955) on one idea: every person is a scientist, forming hypotheses about the world and testing them against experience to anticipate what comes next."
  - Distress, in his view, isn’t a defect. It’s a way of seeing that has stopped being revised. His principle of constructive alternativism holds that there is always another way to see things. He also developed fixed-role therapy, in which people try on a new way of being, like an actor, for a couple of weeks.
concepts:
  - term: Person as scientist
    def: We all build and test theories about life.
  - term: Constructs
    def: The personal categories we use to make sense of people and events.
  - term: Constructive alternativism
    def: There is always another way to see it.
  - term: Fixed-role therapy
    def: Trying on a new character for a while to see what it opens up.
context: "Kelly is the direct ancestor of how the labs describe participants: scientists of their own lives, not patients. We bring the instruments and a room full of fellow researchers. You run the experiments."
exercise:
  title: A two-week role
  time: About 20 minutes to write, then two weeks
  steps:
    - Write a short sketch, in the third person, of a version of you that relates a little differently, for example someone who asks for help easily, or speaks up warmly.
    - Give the character a name if you like. Keep it close enough to you to be believable.
    - For two weeks, play this character in ordinary situations, as an experiment.
    - Notice how people respond and how it feels.
    - At the end, decide what to keep.
  why: This is adapted from Kelly’s fixed-role therapy. Framing the change as a temporary experiment lowers the stakes, so you can test a new way of being and gather real evidence.
