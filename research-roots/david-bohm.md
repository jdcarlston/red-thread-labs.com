tagline: Nothing is truly separate.
who:
  - David Bohm was a theoretical physicist who proposed, in *Wholeness and the Implicate Order* (1980), that beneath the visible world of separate things lies an enfolded, undivided whole that continually unfolds into what we see. That is a physics proposal, and we borrow it here as an image, not as a claim about relationships.
  - "He also developed a practice of group dialogue, described in *On Dialogue* (1996): people suspend their assumptions, watch their own thinking, and build shared meaning together instead of debating. His long conversations with Jiddu Krishnamurti explored the idea that the observer and the observed aren’t separate."
concepts:
  - term: The implicate order
    def: An enfolded wholeness beneath the world of separate things.
  - term: Dialogue
    def: Thinking together, rather than defending positions.
  - term: Suspending assumptions
    def: Holding your views in view, without acting on or defending them.
  - term: Shared meaning
    def: What emerges when a group really listens.
context: Bohm’s dialogue is an ancestor of the listening practices in the labs and our community gatherings. When a room stops debating and starts listening, something gets understood that no one person brought.
exercise:
  title: A dialogue circle
  time: About 45 minutes, with three or more people
  steps:
    - Gather a few people and choose an open question, like “What does it mean to be cherished?”
    - "Agree on a few simple rules: one person speaks at a time, no persuading, no fixing."
    - "When you notice yourself wanting to argue, notice the assumption underneath and say it out loud instead: “I notice I assume…”"
    - Allow silence.
    - At the end, each person shares one thing they understood that they didn’t before.
  why: This follows the spirit of Bohm’s dialogue groups. Naming assumptions instead of defending them lets a group explore together, which tends to produce understanding that debate can’t.
