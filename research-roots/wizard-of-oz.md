  - L. Frank Baum published *The Wonderful Wizard of Oz* in 1900. Through stage and film versions it became one of the most widely shared stories in American culture.
  - In the book, the Scarecrow wants brains, the Tin Woodman wants a heart, and the Cowardly Lion wants courage. Along the road each already shows the quality he believes he lacks. At the end, the good witch tells Dorothy her silver shoes could have carried her home all along.
concepts:

  - term: Heart
    def: The Tin Woodman weeps over a crushed beetle while asking for a heart.
  - term: Mind
    def: The Scarecrow solves problems while believing he has no brains.
  - term: Already home
    def: What we seek is often already ours.
context: "The labs use Oz as a mythic map: courage, heart and mind as three capacities people often believe they lack and already have. JD’s reading of Oz runs through the curriculum. It is a teaching story, not a claim of research."
exercise:
  title: Evidence you already have it
  time: About 20 minutes
  steps:
    - "Pick which you feel you lack most: courage, heart or mind."
    - Write down three specific times you showed it anyway, even in a small way.
    - Read the list back as if it were about someone else. What would you say to them?
    - Write a sentence that names the quality as yours.
    - Notice one moment this week where you use it.
  why: Baum’s companions each display the quality they think they lack. Gathering real evidence from your own life is a practical way to have that same recognition.
