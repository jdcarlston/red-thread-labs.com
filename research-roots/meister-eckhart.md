title: Letting Go
people: Meister Eckhart (c. 1260–c. 1328)
field: Christian mysticism
tagline: What you stop gripping can finally hold you.
who:
  - "Eckhart von Hochheim was a German Dominican theologian and preacher. He taught *Abgeschiedenheit* (detachment) and *Gelassenheit* (releasement): emptying the soul of its attachments, even attachment to its own ideas of God, so that the divine can be born in it."
  - He spoke of the “ground of the soul,” where God and the soul are one. Some of his teachings were condemned after his death, but his sermons became a cornerstone of Western mysticism.
concepts:
  - term: Detachment
    def: Freedom from clinging, not indifference.
  - term: Releasement
    def: Letting things be, rather than forcing them.
  - term: The ground of the soul
    def: A depth in each person where the self and the sacred meet.
  - term: Letting go of ideas
    def: Even our concepts of the sacred can get in the way.
context: "Eckhart’s releasement is close to how the labs understand peace: not a decision you force, but what happens when you stop gripping. Many patterns loosen once we stop fighting them."
exercise:
  title: Releasement
  time: About 15 minutes
  steps:
    - "Sit quietly. Bring to mind something you are gripping: an outcome, an opinion, a person’s approval."
    - Notice where the grip lives in your body.
    - Without trying to make it go away, imagine opening your hand around it, a little.
    - Breathe and let it be as it is for a few minutes.
    - Write what you notice when you’re not gripping.
  why: Eckhart taught releasement as an inner letting-be. Practicing it with one specific attachment makes the idea concrete, and many people find the body softens before the mind does.
