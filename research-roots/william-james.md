who:
  - "William James was an American physician, psychologist and philosopher, often called the father of American psychology. His *Principles of Psychology* (1890) included a chapter on habit that is still one of the clearest accounts of how habits form in the nervous system and how to change them."
  - "He was also a founder of pragmatism, the idea that a belief is true to the degree it works when we live by it. In *The Varieties of Religious Experience* (1902) he studied spiritual experience seriously and with an open mind, judging it by its fruits in people’s lives. His brother was the novelist Henry James."
concepts:
  - term: Habit
    def: Repeated actions carve paths in the nervous system, so that what once took effort starts to run on its own.
  - term: Launch with strength
    def: James advised starting a new habit with as strong and public a beginning as you can manage.
  - term: Never suffer an exception
    def: Each slip, early on, undoes more than a single success builds. Protect a new habit until it holds.
  - term: Act on it right away
    def: Take the first chance to act on a resolution. A good intention that never becomes action weakens the next one.
  - term: Pragmatism
    def: Test an idea by what difference it makes in practice. If it works when you live it, it holds.
context: "James is where our language of habit loops begins. His chapter on habit reads like a field manual for the labs: start strong, repeat, don’t let the old loop sneak back in early, and act before the moment passes. His pragmatism is See · Shift · Prove in philosophy form, and his open-minded study of spiritual experience is the same bridge we build between the practical and the mysterious."
exercise:
  title: Launch one new habit, James’s way
  time: 10 minutes to plan, then two weeks of practice
  steps:
    - Pick one small new behavior you want in your relationships, like a daily thank-you or a pause before you answer.
    - "Launch it strongly: tell someone you trust, and do it for the first time today."
    - "Decide your rule for exceptions: for the next two weeks, there aren’t any."
    - Each day, do it at the first natural chance instead of waiting for the perfect moment.
    - At the end of two weeks, notice what has started to happen on its own.
  why: These are the practical rules James set out in his chapter on habit. They match what later research on habit formation found, that repetition in a stable context is what makes a behavior automatic.
