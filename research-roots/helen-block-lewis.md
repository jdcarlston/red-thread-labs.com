title: Unacknowledged Shame
people: Helen Block Lewis (1913–1987)
field: Psychology · Psychoanalysis
tagline: Shame named out loud loses most of its power.
who:
  - Helen Block Lewis, an American psychologist and psychoanalyst, studied recordings of therapy sessions and found that shame was everywhere and rarely named. In *Shame and Guilt in Neurosis* (1971) she separated shame, where the whole self is judged, from guilt, which focuses on a specific act.
  - "She described shame that goes unacknowledged or bypassed, and “humiliated fury”: shame turning into anger at the person who triggered it."
concepts:
  - term: Self and act
    def: Shame judges the self. Guilt focuses on the behavior.
  - term: Unacknowledged shame
    def: Shame that goes unnamed and drives behavior from underneath.
  - term: Humiliated fury
    def: Shame converting into anger at someone else.
  - term: Bypassed shame
    def: Shame that flickers past so quickly it’s never felt, only acted on.
context: "Lewis is the root of the shame research the labs draw on. Her idea of humiliated fury helps explain many fights: the anger is often protecting a moment of shame. Naming the shame changes the fight."
exercise:
  title: Under the anger
  time: About 15 minutes
  steps:
    - Think of a recent time you got angry quickly, especially at someone close.
    - Replay the moment just before the anger. What did they say or do?
    - "Ask: did I feel small, exposed, foolish, not good enough, even for a second?"
    - "If so, write the shame sentence: “For a moment I felt…”"
    - Consider sharing that sentence, instead of the anger, the next time something similar happens.
  why: Lewis found that shame often goes unnoticed and turns into anger. Looking for the flash of shame just before the anger helps you respond to what actually hurt.
