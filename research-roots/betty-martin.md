tagline: Who is it for, and who is doing? Two questions that change every interaction.
who:
  - "Betty Martin is a former chiropractor, a somatic educator and the creator of the Wheel of Consent. Her book, *The Art of Receiving and Giving: The Wheel of Consent* (2019, with Robyn Dalzen), lays out the model she developed through years of teaching touch and consent workshops."
  - "Her method grew from a simple practice, the 3-Minute Game, in which two people take turns asking “How do you want me to touch you?” and “How do you want to touch me?” She noticed that people found one of the two questions much harder than the other, and built the Wheel to explain why."
concepts:
  - term: Two factors
    def: In any exchange, two things are separate. Who is doing the action, and who it is for. Mixing them up is where resentment and confusion start.
  - term: Four quadrants
    def: "Serving (I do, for you), Taking (I do, for me), Accepting (you do, for me) and Allowing (you do, for you). Each is a real skill, and each needs consent."
  - term: The gift
    def: Inside consent, each quadrant becomes a gift, given or received. Outside it, the same act can turn into its shadow, like self-sacrifice, using or tolerating.
  - term: Your “no” makes your “yes” real
    def: You can only fully agree to something you were also free to decline. Saying what you want, and what you don’t, is how you take part in an exchange.
context: "Martin’s Wheel gives the labs a clear language for one of the hardest parts of connection: knowing what you want, asking for it, and noticing when you’ve been giving something you didn’t really want to give. Many of us learned to serve easily and to take or accept almost never. The Wheel shows the whole map, and that every corner of it can be practiced, starting with something as small as how you want your hand held."
exercise:
  title: The 3-Minute Game, hands only
  time: About 15 minutes, with a partner you trust
  steps:
    - "Agree on the frame first: hands and forearms only, and either of you can say no or stop at any time. A no is welcome information, not a rejection."
    - "Partner A asks: “How do you want me to touch your hand for three minutes?” Partner B takes a moment to notice what they actually want, then asks for it, like slow strokes on the palm or a firm squeeze. A does it, if they’re willing, and B adjusts as they go."
    - "Then A asks: “How do you want to touch my hand for three minutes?” B notices what they would enjoy doing, for themselves, and asks permission. A says yes, or offers a version they’re happy with."
    - Switch so B asks both questions.
    - "Afterward, talk about which question was hardest to answer: what you want done for you, or what you want to do for yourself. Most people find one of them much harder, and that’s the quadrant to practice."
  why: This is the 3-Minute Game, the practice Martin built the Wheel of Consent from. Asking both questions, slowly, separates who is doing from who it’s for, so you can feel the difference between serving, taking, accepting and allowing, and notice which ones you avoid.
