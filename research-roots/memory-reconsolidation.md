title: Memory Reconsolidation

field: Neuroscience · Emotional learning
tagline: A memory reopened is a memory that can be updated.
who:
  - In [a 2000 study](https://doi.org/10.1038/35021052), Karim Nader and colleagues showed that when a fear memory is recalled, it briefly becomes unstable and has to be stored again. During that window, it can be changed.
  - Bruce Ecker and colleagues carried the idea into therapy in *Unlocking the Emotional Brain* (2012). To change a deep emotional learning, they argue, you bring it up and, while it is active, have a vivid experience that contradicts it. [A 2015 review](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0140525X14000041) proposed reconsolidation as a common ingredient across effective therapies. The animal findings are strong. How best to use it with people is still being studied.
concepts:
  - term: Recall opens the memory
    def: Bringing up an emotional learning makes it briefly changeable.
  - term: The update window
    def: In that window, a contradicting experience can revise it.
  - term: Change versus coping
    def: Updating the old learning, rather than managing it forever.
  - term: Mismatch
    def: The new experience has to genuinely contradict the old expectation.
context: Reconsolidation is at the heart of how the labs aim to change patterns, not just manage them. We help people bring up an old expectation and, in the same moment, have a real experience with other people that doesn’t fit it.
exercise:
  title: The rule and its exception
  time: About 20 minutes
  steps:
    - Write one old rule you live by about relationships, like “If I ask for what I want, people leave.” Choose a mild one.
    - Let yourself feel how true it seems. Notice where it lives in your body.
    - "Now recall a specific, real moment that broke the rule: someone stayed, said yes, or came back."
    - "Hold both at once: the old rule and the real exception. Notice what happens to the feeling of the rule."
    - Write a new sentence that includes what you now know.
  why: "Ecker’s approach describes this sequence: bring up the old learning, then experience something that contradicts it while it is active. Doing it with a mild rule and a real memory is a small version of the process the research describes."
