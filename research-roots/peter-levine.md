tagline: The body can finish what the moment interrupted, gently, a little at a time.
who:
  - "Peter Levine developed Somatic Experiencing from an observation: animals in the wild face life-threatening danger, then shake, move and return to normal, while people often override that release and stay stuck in fight, flight or freeze. In *Waking the Tiger* (1997) he described trauma as survival energy that never got to finish."
  - Healing, in his approach, comes less from retelling the story than from slowly letting those interrupted responses complete in the body, in small doses. [A 2017 randomized trial](https://doi.org/10.1002/jts.22189) found Somatic Experiencing reduced PTSD symptoms compared to a waitlist.
concepts:
  - term: Titration
    def: Touching difficult sensation in small, manageable doses.
  - term: Pendulation
    def: Moving attention back and forth between activation and calm.
  - term: Completion
    def: Letting an interrupted protective response finish in the body.
  - term: Felt sense
    def: Close attention to raw body sensation as the place change happens.
context: "Levine gives the labs a way to meet strong reactions without flooding people: small doses, slow pace, and attention to the body. It’s why our practices start with sensation, move slowly, and always include a way to come back to calm."
exercise:
  title: Pendulation
  time: About 10 minutes
  steps:
    - "Sit comfortably and notice your body. Find one place that feels easy or neutral: hands, feet, the back of your legs on the chair."
    - Now find a place with mild tension or discomfort. Choose something small, not your hardest spot.
    - Rest your attention on the easy place for a few breaths, then on the tense place for a breath or two.
    - "Move back and forth slowly several times. Notice any changes: warmth, a deep breath, a small movement."
    - End on the easy place. Look around the room before you get up.
  why: Pendulation and titration are core moves in Somatic Experiencing. Moving between ease and mild activation teaches the nervous system that it can touch discomfort and return, which builds capacity without overwhelm.
