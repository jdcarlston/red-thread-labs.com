title: The Neuroscience of Pair Bonding

tagline: The spark is chemistry. The bond is what you build with it.
who:
  - Larry Young and Thomas Insel studied the prairie vole, one of the few rodents that forms lasting pair bonds. They found that when voles mate, oxytocin and vasopressin act in the brain’s reward areas and tie the pleasure of dopamine to one particular partner. Change those receptors and you change whether the animal bonds. Their [2001 review](https://doi.org/10.1038/35053579) laid out this neurobiology of attachment.
  - In *The Chemistry Between Us* (2012), Young and Brian Alexander argue that human bonding runs on related circuitry. The animal findings are strong. How far they carry over to people is still being worked out.
concepts:
  - term: Oxytocin and vasopressin
    def: Hormones that help turn attraction into attachment.
  - term: Reward binding
    def: Dopamine says “this feels good.” The bonding hormones add “with this one.”
  - term: Receptors, not fate
    def: Small differences in brain wiring change the capacity to bond.
  - term: Shared circuitry
    def: Bonding uses reward systems that are old and widely shared among mammals.
context: "This research supports a core idea in our neuroattraction work: bonding is learned in the reward system, and learning responds to experience. The rush of a spark gets attached to whoever is there when the circuit fires. That helps explain why we can bond to people who aren’t good for us, and why the pattern can change."
exercise:
  title: Where does the spark come from?
  time: About 25 minutes, written
  steps:
    - List three people you felt a strong pull toward, past or present.
    - "For each, note what was happening when the pull was strongest: newness, uncertainty, chasing, reunion after distance, or steady warmth."
    - Circle anything that shows up more than once.
    - Now list one or two people who are reliably good to you. Notice whether they give you the same rush.
    - Write a sentence about what you’d like the spark to attach to from here on.
  why: The vole studies show bonding is a learning process in the reward system. In people, rewards that come unpredictably tend to feel especially gripping. Seeing which conditions have charged your attractions helps you notice them as they happen and choose where you invest.
