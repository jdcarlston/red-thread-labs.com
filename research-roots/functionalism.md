who:
  - "In the 1960s, philosopher Hilary Putnam proposed functionalism: a mental state is defined by what it does, its role among inputs, outputs and other states, not by what it’s made of. Jerry Fodor developed it into the computational theory of mind in *The Language of Thought* (1975)."
  - "Douglas Hofstadter gave it a poetic form in *Gödel, Escher, Bach* (1979) and *I Am a Strange Loop* (2007): the “I” as a self-referring pattern, real as a pattern rather than as a thing. Whether mind can be separated from the brain as cleanly as software from hardware is still debated."
concepts:
  - term: Functional role
    def: A mental state is what it does.
  - term: Multiple realizability
    def: The same role could be filled by different physical systems.
  - term: Strange loop
    def: The self as a pattern that arises when a system models itself.
  - term: The limit
    def: The software analogy is useful, and also contested.
context: "Functionalism supports a useful question in the labs: what role is this habit playing? If a pattern is defined by its role, something else can fill the role."
exercise:
  title: Same role, new player
  time: About 15 minutes
  steps:
    - Pick a habit, like late-night scrolling, over-working or picking fights.
    - "Describe its role: what comes in (the trigger), what goes out (the result), and what it connects to."
    - Brainstorm three other things that could play the same role.
    - Choose one and try it in place of the habit three times this week.
    - Notice whether the role gets filled.
  why: Functionalism defines mental states by their role, not their substance. Seeing a habit by its role makes it clear that a different action could do the same job.
