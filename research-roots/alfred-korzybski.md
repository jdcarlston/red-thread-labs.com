who:
  - "Alfred Korzybski, a Polish-American engineer and philosopher, developed general semantics in *Science and Sanity* (1933): a way of noticing how our nervous systems, through language, build our experience of reality."
  - His best-known line, “the map is not the territory,” became a founding idea of NLP and of much systems thinking. A map is useful when its structure matches the territory, but it is never the territory. He offered simple habits, like dating and indexing words, to keep our maps honest.
concepts:
  - term: Map and territory
    def: Our knowledge is always a map, never the thing itself.
  - term: Levels of abstraction
    def: Words and ideas sit at different levels, and mixing them breeds confusion.
  - term: Indexing and dating
    def: John in 2015 is not John in 2026. Neither is you.
  - term: Silence on the objective level
    def: Some things can only be reached by pointing and being quiet.
context: "Korzybski is the root of a phrase you’ll hear constantly in the labs: the map. Patterns are maps of what love and people are like, drawn long ago. Our work is updating the map by going back to the territory: real people, in a real room."
exercise:
  title: Date and index
  time: About 10 minutes
  steps:
    - "Write three sentences you believe about yourself or someone close: “I’m bad at conflict.” “My mother never listens.”"
    - "Add a date to the subject: “Me in 2019 was bad at conflict.” “My mother, in 2008, didn’t listen.”"
    - Ask whether the sentence is still true today, every time.
    - Write one specific counterexample if you can find one.
    - Rewrite the sentence so it fits the territory better.
  why: Korzybski proposed dating and indexing as habits to keep language from freezing people into fixed categories. Adding a date makes visible that people, including you, change.
