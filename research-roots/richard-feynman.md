people: Richard Feynman (1918–1988)

tagline: Everything that could happen contributes.
who:
  - Richard Feynman helped found quantum electrodynamics and invented the [path-integral approach](https://doi.org/10.1103/RevModPhys.20.367) to quantum mechanics. In it, a particle is treated as taking every possible path at once, each contributing to the outcome, and what we observe emerges from how those paths combine and cancel.
  - "His book *QED: The Strange Theory of Light and Matter* (1985) explained it to a general audience. We use the idea here only as a way of thinking about choices, not as a claim that the mind works like a particle."
concepts:
  - term: Sum over histories
    def: Every possible path contributes to what actually happens.
  - term: Interference
    def: Paths can reinforce or cancel each other.
  - term: The actual
    def: What we see is what survives all the adding up.
  - term: A way of thinking
    def: Here, a metaphor for how many small influences shape a result.
context: As a metaphor, Feynman’s sum over paths is a reminder that any outcome is shaped by many small, unseen influences. In relationships, the habits that cancel out the good, criticism, contempt, distraction, are often the ones to change.
exercise:
  title: All the ways it could go
  time: About 15 minutes
  steps:
    - Pick a situation coming up that you’re anxious about.
    - List at least eight ways it could go, from wonderful to awful to boring.
    - Notice which outcome you’ve been treating as the only one.
    - Next to each, note one small thing that would make it more or less likely.
    - Choose two small actions that tilt things toward the outcomes you want.
  why: Anxiety tends to fixate on one outcome. Listing many possibilities, a practice used in cognitive therapy, loosens that fixation and points to small levers you can actually pull.
