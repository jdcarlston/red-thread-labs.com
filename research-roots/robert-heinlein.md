title: To Grok

tagline: To understand so fully that, for a while, you become what you understand.
who:
  - "Robert A. Heinlein was one of the most successful science-fiction writers of the twentieth century. *Stranger in a Strange Land* (1961) introduced the Martian word “grok”: to understand something so completely that the observer becomes part of what is observed."
  - The novel became a touchstone for the 1960s counterculture, with its themes of communal living and radical honesty in relationships, and “grok” entered the dictionary.
concepts:
  - term: Grok
    def: Total, inside-out understanding.
  - term: Water brother
    def: A bond of deep trust and sharing in the novel.
  - term: Radical honesty
    def: Relationships without pretense.
  - term: Return
    def: Fully entering another’s world, then coming back carrying what you found.
context: "Heinlein’s word gives the labs a name for the deepest form of stepping into another person’s experience: not just understanding them from outside, but temporarily living inside their view, and then returning to your own."
exercise:
  title: Grok something they love
  time: An hour or more
  steps:
    - "Choose someone close to you and something they love that you don’t understand: a game, a craft, a kind of music, a team."
    - Ask them to show you, and spend at least an hour inside it.
    - Ask questions from curiosity, not judgment.
    - Try to feel why it matters to them.
    - Afterward, tell them one thing you now understand about them.
  why: Grokking means understanding from inside. Spending real time inside someone else’s passion, with curiosity, is a practical way to move toward that kind of understanding.
