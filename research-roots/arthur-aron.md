tagline: If strangers can build closeness on purpose, so can you.
who:
  - "Arthur Aron, a social psychologist, developed with Elaine Aron the self-expansion model of love: we’re drawn to relationships that enlarge who we are, and falling in love is a rapid expansion of the self."
  - In [“The Experimental Generation of Interpersonal Closeness” (1997)](https://doi.org/10.1177/0146167297234003) he and colleagues showed that pairs of strangers taking turns answering increasingly personal questions, the source of the famous 36 Questions, felt real closeness in about 45 minutes. He also worked with Helen Fisher on brain imaging of romantic love.
concepts:
  - term: Self-expansion
    def: We seek relationships that grow the self.
  - term: The 36 Questions
    def: Gradually deeper, mutual sharing that builds closeness.
  - term: Taking turns
    def: Closeness comes from both people being seen, not from time alone.
  - term: Shared novelty
    def: Doing new, mildly challenging things together can renew closeness.
context: "Aron’s work is the proof that closeness can be built, not only stumbled into. The labs use the same structure: gradual, mutual, paced sharing in a safe container."
exercise:
  title: A short set of questions
  time: About 20 minutes, with one other person
  steps:
    - "Choose someone you’d like to know better: a friend, a partner, a family member."
    - "Take turns answering these, each of you answering every question: What would make a perfect day for you? What are you most grateful for in your life? If you could change one thing about how you were raised, what would it be?"
    - Listen fully. Don’t fix or advise.
    - Then each share one thing you appreciate about the other.
    - Afterward, notice whether you feel closer, and what made the difference.
  why: These questions come from the first set in Aron’s 1997 closeness study. The research showed that closeness grows from gradual, reciprocal self-disclosure, so the turn-taking matters as much as the questions.
