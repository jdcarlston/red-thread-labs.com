  - "Simon Wardley is a British researcher and strategist who developed Wardley Mapping. Unhappy with business frameworks that lacked a sense of position and movement, he created maps that work like a general’s view of the terrain: where things are, how they’re changing, and what options exist from where you stand."
  - A Wardley map plots the activities needed to meet a user’s need against how evolved each one is, from new and uncertain to everyday commodity. He has published his book on mapping freely online under a Creative Commons license.

context: "Wardley Mapping is part of JD’s engineering leadership practice and of the strategy work Red Thread Labs does with teams. The same instinct runs through the labs: make the map visible, then choose your move."
exercise:
  title: Map one need
  time: About 30 minutes
  steps:
    - At the top of a page, write a need you or your team are trying to meet.
    - Below it, list what’s needed to meet it, then what those things depend on, down a few layers.
    - Draw a line across the page from “new and uncertain” on the left to “everyday and standard” on the right.
    - Place each item on that line according to how mature it is.
    - Look for something you’re custom-building that could be standard, or something new you’re treating as settled.
  why: This is a simplified Wardley map. Placing parts of a problem by how evolved they are shows where to innovate and where to use what already exists.
