who:
  - "The Sphere of Somata is JD Carlston’s map of the whole felt body as one field you can stand inside and move through. Its smallest units come from the Greek for “bodies.” A soma is one dimension of one sense, like brightness, weight, pitch, temperature or felt age. Its somata are the readings along that scale. Each one is something you can locate, name and turn."
  - "It builds on what NLP and the Destination Method call submodalities, and on the research into interoception, our sense of what’s happening inside the body. The map holds five senses: visual, auditory, smell, taste, and the felt body, which gathers the interior channels like core felt sense, balance, interoception, temperature, pain and the sense of time. Its companion, the [Sphere of Emotions](/library/sphere-of-emotions/), reads what the sensations add up to. An emotion, in this work, is somata with a story attached."
concepts:
  - term: Soma and somata
    def: The atoms of felt experience. A soma is one dimension of one sense, small enough to notice and move. Its somata are the readings along its scale.
  - term: The Sphere of Somata
    def: The whole felt body as one field, every sense and every somata held together. A territory to move through, not a state to reach.
  - term: Somata plus story
    def: The same tight chest can read as fear, excitement or love depending on the story laid over it. Work the sensation and the story both.
  - term: Turning a somata
    def: Shifting one quality, like heavier to lighter or closer to farther, until the nervous system has more room.
  - term: Brave and Within
    def: It serves the Brave rotation, a regulated body that can risk, and the Within rotation, inhabiting a sensation from the inside.
framework:
  name: The five senses of the Sphere
  intro: Each sense is a channel the charge can travel on. Find the channel first, then the somata inside it.
  items:
    - name: Visual
      def: Brightness, color, size, distance, focus and movement in what you see or picture.
    - name: Auditory
      def: Volume, pitch, tempo, direction and tone in what you hear, inside or out.
    - name: Olfactory
      def: Smells, and how strong, sweet, sharp or familiar they are.
    - name: Gustatory
      def: Tastes, including the ones that come with a memory or a feeling.
    - name: Kinesthetic, the felt body
      def: Touch, weight, pressure, temperature, balance, heartbeat, breath, gut, pain and felt age. The interior channels live here.
context: "Most of what pulls us, toward people and away from them, is written in the body before it’s written in words. The Sphere of Somata gives people a way to find that writing and change it. It’s how the labs take the Brave and Within rotations out of ideas and into the body. The evidence is honest about its limits. Research supports interoceptive awareness and finer emotional distinctions as aids to regulation ([Craig, 2002](https://doi.org/10.1038/nrn894); [Kashdan, Barrett & McKnight, 2015](https://doi.org/10.1177/0963721414550708)). Submodality techniques themselves come from clinical practice and haven’t been tested well in controlled trials ([Sturt and colleagues, 2012](https://doi.org/10.3399/bjgp12X658287))."
exercise:
  title: Find it, name it, turn it
  time: About 15 minutes
  steps:
    - Bring to mind a moment that still carries a charge, mild to start.
    - Scan the five senses and find the channel carrying the charge. It’s often the felt body, like the chest, throat or gut.
    - "Name the somata as precisely as you can: where it is, how big, how heavy, what temperature, whether it moves, how old it feels."
    - Pick one quality and turn it slowly along its scale, like heavier to lighter or tighter to looser. Notice what happens as it moves.
    - Notice whether the story about the moment changes too, and write down one thing that’s different.
  why: An emotion is sensation with a story attached. Naming the sensation precisely is a form of interoceptive awareness and emotional differentiation, both linked to better regulation. Turning one quality is borrowed from NLP and Destination Method submodality work, which gives the nervous system a small, safe change to notice.
