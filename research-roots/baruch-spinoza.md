tagline: The feeling that runs you is one you haven’t yet understood.
who:
  - Baruch Spinoza, a Dutch lens-grinder excommunicated from his community for his ideas, wrote the *Ethics* (published after his death in 1677) as a chain of geometric proofs. He argued that there is one reality, God or Nature, and everything is a part of it.
  - "His central practical claim: we are bound by our emotions only while we understand them confusedly. Once we form a clear idea of an emotion, it stops being something that merely happens to us (*Ethics*, Part V). Freedom isn’t the absence of feeling. It’s understanding feeling."
concepts:
  - term: Passions and actions
    def: An emotion we suffer becomes one we act from when we understand it.
  - term: Clear ideas
    def: Understanding the causes of a feeling loosens its grip.
  - term: Conatus
    def: Every being’s effort to keep going and flourish.
  - term: One substance
    def: Everything is part of one nature.
context: "Spinoza is a philosophical spine under the labs’ emotional practice: you don’t have to fight your feelings, you have to understand them. Modern research on naming emotions echoes his insight."
exercise:
  title: A clear idea of a feeling
  time: About 15 minutes
  steps:
    - "Pick a feeling that has been running you lately: jealousy, resentment, longing, dread."
    - "Describe it as precisely as you can: where you feel it, how strong, when it started."
    - "Write what you believe that makes this feeling make sense: “I feel this because I believe…”"
    - Write what caused it, as far as you can tell, without blaming anyone.
    - Read it back and notice whether the feeling has changed, even slightly.
  why: "Spinoza argued that forming a clear and distinct idea of an emotion changes our relationship to it. Modern affect-labeling research points the same way: understanding a feeling helps regulate it."
