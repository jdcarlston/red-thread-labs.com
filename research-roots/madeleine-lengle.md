  - "Madeleine L’Engle was an American writer best known for *A Wrinkle in Time* (1962). In its sequel, *A Wind in the Door* (1973), she introduced “kything”: a wordless communion in which one person is fully present to another, being to being."
  - She took the word from older Scots, where to kythe means to make known or to appear. Where Heinlein’s “grok” names total understanding, kything names a chosen, quiet, relational presence.
concepts:
  - term: Kything
    def: Silent, chosen presence with another.
  - term: To make visible
    def: "The old meaning of the word: to show, to appear."
  - term: Naming
    def: In her story, truly naming someone helps them become themselves.
  - term: Communion
    def: Connection that doesn’t depend on words.
context: "The labs borrow L’Engle’s word for one of our quietest practices: sitting with another person in silent, attentive presence. It’s often where the deepest connection happens."
exercise:
  title: Five minutes of kything
  time: About ten minutes, with another person
  steps:
    - Sit facing someone you trust, at a comfortable distance.
    - Agree to be silent for five minutes. You can look at each other or soften your gaze.
    - Simply be present to them. When your mind wanders, come back to them.
    - Notice what you feel, and what passes between you without words.
    - Afterward, each share one word for the experience.
  why: L’Engle described kything as presence beyond words. Shared silent attention is also a recognized practice in contemplative traditions and in some therapies, and many people find it surprisingly intimate.
