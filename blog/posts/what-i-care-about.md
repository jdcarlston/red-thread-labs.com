### The Science and Art of What and How We Love

* * *

I’ve been reverse engineering and debugging and nerding out about the concept of love for as long as I can remember.

![The Red Thread Labs logo: a red triangle inside a white triangle on a black circle, above the words Red Thread Labs.]({{ "img/blog/what-i-care-about/01-1-2JqccRn2Hv5jzoBGsUthJw.png" | media }})
*Labs to Love More*

As a child I was fascinated by the sacred, by hidden knowledge, by people relating with one another, by understanding the beginnings of things and how they came to be. By what it meant when people told me they loved me and how that got expressed in various ways.

[bell hooks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bell_hooks) speaks of love so elegantly. She builds on M. Scott Peck’s definition, who was building on Thomas Aquinas’ work. They gave me my favorite definition of love:

> Love is "The will to extend one’s self for the purpose of nurturing one’s own or another’s spiritual growth." —M. Scott Peck

### Meaningful Action

How our minds create meaning around our experiences informs the choices we make each day. Those meaning making systems define the feelings we have, the intent behind the language we speak, and the actions we take to invest in the spiritual growth of an other. Where we place our identity will determine which Other. I use these words with specific definitions that are crucial to break down.

> ”I AM **is** The Way, The Truth, and The Light.”

If love is an action then loving can be practiced in practical ways. It can be developed as a skill and tested and improved upon. It can be studied directly, both in the comparative subjective and the objective.

> “do good for him who does good that he may do good” — The “Peasant’s Complaint,” a popular poem on the conflict of Maat and corruption, Egypt c. 2000 B.C.

![A printed copy of the poem Desiderata in a plastic sleeve, hanging on a stainless-steel refrigerator door.]({{ "img/blog/what-i-care-about/02-1-Pmul5ieOarCjblCC3Rb-AQ.jpeg" | media }})
*Max Ehrmann’s Poem, often misattributed. it’s so important to consider the source.*

> “The essential immoral act is the inability to see other people correctly. The essential moral act is being able to cast just and loving attention on another person — looking at others with patience and discerning regard. ‘Love is knowledge of the individual.’” — Iris Murdoch

It does to keep in mind that all knowledge is process in action.

* * *

i am driven by a desire to connect to people and consider the source through time, with those who have been on their own journeys, observing the world and what it means to be human. To love deeply and profoundly. To find the divine in all things.

I wanted to be an archaeologist because of these interests as a child.

In high school I realized I wanted to create the future and help myself and other people live in more coherence. I started studying chemistry and materials and how they work together.

I learned and taught children sports, rode horses, how to ski and swim and so much more at my grandparents’ schools. I worked in QA testing in high school. I tutored people in math.

I learned about business and sold software until I learned how to create websites and write code.

I have and was taught and took classes and learned to be an engineer, to understand visualisation of data, information, knowledge, wisdom, and flow. To create music and art and poetry in form and function. To sing.

I have taken classes around the country in what has interested me. I spent a lot of time in one on one sessions learning new lexicons, in libraries and reading papers and books, following the thread of joy and love and learning from the deep experts in my life.

And I got married and had children. I did all I could to provide for my family and the people in my life, to create stability and a home filled with love. Often under some pretty large constraints.

Sometimes I detached in the day-to-day, but the thread was always there.

I studied psychology and the mind and how my thoughts, beliefs and actions impacted myself and others.

I studied telecommunications, the Internet and computers, because I wanted to understand how signal flowed — how it can be used to improve communication with one another, to build community outside of time and space, and help find people who cared and were curious and passionate about life in exciting ways.

A.I. is interesting and an aggregator of things that are driving decisions being made today. It can’t love.

* * *

But it’s love that brings me home.

I feel driven to create Love Labs with my husband and friends in a way that tugs at my heart strings. Its been my life for so long. Testing and experimenting with connection and compassion in action.

I think what we want to build is so compelling and freeing and fun.

I hope others will join. Learning what works and what doesn’t. Building the skills together, in a safe observational container free of judgment and shame.

Identifying blindspots in the care we show others through reflective communication and reduction of egoic projection.

Reducing suffering, expressing authentic truth, building community, in-person. Learning how to be better partners, parents, and souls and connecting the internal work to the outer expression the the choices we apply, wear, and present. Connecting action to word to belief to meaning and memory that is stored throughout the body, spirit, and soul.

There’s a vision of this that I want to build. I can help others get where they want to go, but following this thread upon it’s circuitous path, ultimately is my life’s work.
