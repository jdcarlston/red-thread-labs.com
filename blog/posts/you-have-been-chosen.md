Being chosen means that *You, as a sovereign being,* choose the life *You* want. You choose yourself every time. You listen to your heart, your body, and you align with your mind.

You may now follow your own internal compass. You follow the path of greatest joy. You refuse to push your needs onto others, or allow others to push their needs onto you. You become the person who is being reacted to by sharing your truth. People then sort themselves into and out of your life. Choosing yourself allows you to act with a force of will and purpose.

In choosing myself *I care more about improving myself than proving myself to others*. I regularly reflect on how I’m feeling, on what I want, and who I want to be. I make small iterative changes as well big life-changing decisions. I seek to maintain my equilibrium, my independence and my self-worth. I grow in conscious awareness of my nature. I give myself freedom to act in accordance with my best nature. I find joy in increasing my reasoning and practicing skills.

## Being Ungrounded

Choosing myself means holding my boundaries and listening to my body when it feels ungrounded.

- When I feel lightheaded
- When my stomach hurts, drops or twists
- When I feel numb or listless
- When my jaw or fists clench
- When I feel heavy, tense or faint
- When I get sick or tired
- When I feel stuck, routine, or complacent
- When I get angry, sad, nervous or upset
- When I start questioning myself or others
- When I feel overwhelmed
- When I am unable to be curious or playful
- When I start telling myself I am failing or losing out
- When my emotional or physical state changes for the worse
- Generally, when I feel something I don’t like

All these are signs of a [boundary being crossed](https://thedewdrop.org/2020/05/18/the-dexterous-butcher-zhuangzi/), a need going unmet, or a shift in perspective being necessary. This is a time to pause, to slow down, and to pay attention. I may want to spend time alone in order to understand a situation that does not yet make sense. I want to imagine and consider as many decision options before me as possible. It helps to write them down in a journal. Here’s a [decision template](https://docs.google.com/document/d/1eRQlW0smTLvNS62F4PybN58KvQxo0ovjf_C8yMES2IQ/edit?usp=sharing) adopted from the ADR framework.

## Grounding

Grounding is getting in touch with your senses, with your body, and with your feet on the earth. I am grounded when:

- When there’s a pleasant warmth in my core
- When there’s a calm behind my eyes and within my mind
- When I feel settled, relaxed, safe, and well-resourced
- When I feel excited, happy, or calm
- When I am in a state of flow or a learning zone

Becoming grounded is me choosing myself in a difficult (complicated or complex) situation.

## Challenges

I may find this challenging if:

- I feel rushed or pressured to make a decision
- I can’t think of any acceptable options to act or find comfort
- I am not taking responsibility for my own feelings
- I am choosing to remain in an unhealthy situation
- I feel incapable of creating some kind of change
- I am trying to balance my own needs with those of others

## The Myth of Merging

The myth of others “choosing me” is based on their biased context and understanding, on their perceptual universe. They can’t really choose *me*, except in some limited form that exists between our shared realities.

![Single-item inclusion of Other in the Self, Arthur Aron]({{ "img/blog/you-have-been-chosen/03-0-LDWXn52RM4G6PnGw.png" | media }})
*[*Single-item inclusion of Other in the Self, Arthur Aron*](https://sparqtools.org/mobility-measure/inclusion-of-other-in-the-self-ios-scale/)*

*Humans are inclined to believe they understand Others.* You might think you know what they think and feel without even asking. If you start asking you’ll find out your personal narrative about what’s going on inside their minds is often very different and incorrect.

The mental maps, the impressions we build, are not accurate reflections of reality. [Samskara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samskara_%28Indian_philosophy%29) can imprison us. You may have created a false belief. You may think you know who they are and what they want. Your mental map is now boinked. Regardless of if the other person, or group, believes similarly, you have moved into the realm of dependency. The maps you are using to make choices, that you rely on, are based in a false reality that you have no control over. *Dependency, by its nature, creates adversarial relationships.*

## Maintaining Integrity

Choosing ourselves, respecting our boundaries, is an act of maintaining our personal integrity. Any actions taken from an ungrounded position compromise who I want to be. I will be acting from a weaker perspective, and I will diminish any relationship at play. *I have to act from the best version of myself to become who I want to be.*

Unrealistic expectations and misunderstandings placed on another person or organization (let alone yourself) can be toxic.

Being kind, expressing gratitude that others are a part of your life, accepting the “No” given from friends, family and partners are all essential forms of respect. **Respect is the action of asking for and getting information from another without coercion or manipulation.** Respecting agency and full consent is essential. *Relationships are defined by the* ***agreements*** *we’ve made with others.*

In other words, I must state what I feel, need and want. I must identify my own pain points. And then listen to the feelings of others — *without the intention of trying to cause any change in those feelings*! When you use the expression of emotions as control mechanisms, when you manipulate and strategize rather than speak from your center, you are working from an adversarial position via dependency. Respect can increase access and privilege. Disrespect decreases it.

## Perceptual Positions

The most resilient people are those able to easily imagine and embody at least three different perceptual perspectives. Being stuck or unaware of any one of these will create a more rigid and inaccurate world view.

- Self
- Other
- Observer

## Rejection

Sharing your truth can lead to people rejecting you. Rejection is an opportunity to make different choices and choose a new path. What we term rejection is fundamentally a disempowering narrative. When we believe people reject us it’s important to understand their actions are more about having a different universe of experience and perspective. It is *never* personal.

If I perceive that I’m not being chosen in some specific instance, I have the option to examine the context and what is causing that belief. I can determine if there are other ways to choose myself, to understand what is happening and find people that are willing to interact with me in the ways I want.

Rejection creates a certain physiological state in our brain and in our body, but our *mind* is what determines if that state will change. When we change the impression we have we change the map we’ve created to represent what is happening. We change our state.

## Scenarios

**Scenario 1:**

I remember being picked last for a team in elementary school. I was new to the school and was quite shy. As everyone else in class was picked I felt myself get more and more antsy. I felt embarrassed and ashamed that no one wanted me on their team. The designated captains had no idea that I could play. I felt sad and hurt as I stepped into the field, but tried to show people what I could do. I had a vast number of options to choose from in how I acted when I was picked last.

One way I healed that wound was to approach different people and play the game with them later on at recess. I also explored practicing the skills alone at home and on the field.

I focused on developing the skills necessary to play the game and built new connections with others. If I had been aware of it I could have moved between the different perceptual positions to get a better idea of how to act with even more power and authentic reason.

**Scenario 2:**

Let’s say I really want to go out to dinner. My partner does not. I ask my partner and they say “No.” Immediately I feel a painful ache in my stomach and feel heat rising in my face and intense sensations pulse through my body. I feel a desire to act and to express myself out loud.

I can choose to react immediately or I can pause to listen more carefully to what’s happening inside me, as well as with my partner.

If I react immediately based on the shift in my physical state I might only see a few options before me. Flight, flight, freeze.

If I pause I can see other possible paths.

I can identify my *pain points*. I might feel hungry and realize that the story I’m telling myself is one of being rejected. I might realize I don’t want to go to dinner alone. I might realize that the myth I’m living in makes cooking at home sound like work that I must do for others. I can even use something called [parts work](https://www.google.com/amp/s/www.psychologytoday.com/intl/blog/making-the-whole-beautiful/202202/how-parts-work-helps-us-get-to-know-ourselves%3famp) to query the different areas of my body that are in pain in order to understand them better.

I can identify my *needs and desires*. I have a primary need for food. I have secondary needs for relaxation at a restaurant and connection with my partner. I might want a chance to connect with my partner somewhere that feels more neutral. I might have other needs that might feel like a storm within my body that I must identify and validate in order to feel grounded.

I give myself the opportunity to self-soothe, to speak to my best nature and those of my partner, but only when I *know* my actions will be respectful to all parties. I must listen to shift my context, current state and beliefs.

I can imagine each of the different perceptual positions as well to get a better idea of the whole situation and empathize with my partner.

When I have chosen myself I can choose my reaction from the many options that I lay out. Knowing myself better helps me soothe my pain, to meet my needs, and reach for my desires. Again, this is a key to acting with integrity.

## Framing

When a decision is required, much of what determines the options being made is the frame from which I view the space. Choosing myself, pausing, and re-evaluating when I believe I’m being rejected allows me to reframe from a position of greater power.

## Identifying Potential Options

- I can choose to get upset with my partner, thinking this means I must work to eat, go hungry, and go without having a connection with them. (Anger and fear be used as tools to browbeat and create submission. Can you see how this is a form of disrespect and manipulation?)
- I can go alone and get my needs for food and relaxation met and connect with my partner at another time.
- I can choose to see this as an opportunity to get to know my partner better. We can discuss their needs, desires, and experiences with them. This might mean that I defer my other needs and fix food for myself at home.
- I can identify that I likely need to express the hurt I felt at some point so they can know me better as well.
- I can find another person to go to dinner with to build a greater connection with them.

Once I’ve made the options I can take and share a decision that more rigorously serves my own needs.

Throughout it’s important to remember that we are an interdependent species. We must be able to share our feelings and needs without the fear that the relationship will end.

We have needs for connection with others. Our well-being depends on the well-being of others. *If I am suffering it is up to me to determine how to resolve that.* It is up to me to ask for and find help when I need it. It is up to me to create and support systems that help others.

Acting wisely requires acknowledging our interdependent needs, wants and pain points without shame.

## Modes of Inquiry

As I’m making options and discovering more about myself I start asking myself probing questions and actively listening for the answers that come from the parts of my body that feel most sensational. I write out my thoughts and start journaling the answers that come bubbling up from the depths of my being. I re-work the maps contained in my mind:

**Short Term**

- What are the feelings in my body at this moment?
- Where are they located?
- Am I in conflict? What parts seem to be in conflict?
- What is the gift in these sensations?
- What needs do I have at this moment?
- What do I want to happen?
- What does choosing myself look like right now?
- How do I feel when I do this particular thing?
- How do I feel when I think this particular thought?

**Mid-Term**

- What might others be feeling in this situation?
- What would be an outside observer’s take on this situation?
- What actions make the most sense to me when I embody my ideal self?
- What do my daily habits look like lately?
- Are there any habits I want to keep or discard?
- What structure would be most harmonious and life-giving considering my own personal development, the needs and desires of others, and other life circumstances?
- Can I make a decision as freely as possible from the pressures of social, biological, biographical, and cultural conditioning?
- What does this situation look like to the interested observer?

**Long-Term**

- What are my goals, reasons and purpose in acting, speaking, or thinking?
- What myth am I living right now? What myths do I want to live by?
- What are my values in this situation?
- What are my long-term goals and life’s purpose?
- What is the ideal vision for my life?
- If I were the hero in a movie, what would the best outcome for my story be?
- What does this situation look like in a broader context?

## Truth-o-Meter

## Finding Equilibrium

Equilibrium can be found in pausing to contemplate, in ritualizing self, other, and observer-awareness, in caring for myself and my environment. I can journal about my needs and wants and my options to get them. I can find people who want to help create the feelings and experiences I am seeking.

When I feel a desire to be chosen I believe it comes from me not fully choosing myself. This means I need to check in with my body or my mind. The desire is rooted in a sense of lack and a sense of scarcity. Desires like this indicate that I haven’t been choosing myself. I may not be accepting the limitations of others unequivocally. Realizing this points to a need to choose a new path and new options.

Desires like this lie within dependency and adversity, regret and sorrow. I want to choose options most likely to create the greatest joy, when they become available to me. I’m giving myself permission to act in my own best interests and to speak my truth. *The truth is true regardless of the outcome it creates.*

In order to grow I need to experience different types of relating to others and the world. I need to experience being dependent, independent, co-dependent, and interdependent.

Those who can move between all of these forms of dependence with ease can readily transition and transform when necessary. They can move from adversarial to felicitous paradigms of relating.

## Sharing Your Truth

To recap, choosing yourself means identifying and understanding your truth. Your truth might change with new information and you might be tempted to assign a narrative to that truth before you have a coherent understanding around what is happening in your body.

As you are beginning to understand something about yourself you can utilize the tools I’ve provided above. Once you have gathered the information through a self-discovery process you might want to break out of your navel gazing and start sharing your desires, needs and pain points with others.

> …our brains are exceptionally good at creating coherent (but not necessarily true) stories that deceive us.

> Torres, Teresa. Continuous Discovery Habits: Discover Products that Create Customer Value and Business Value (p. 76)

The narrative is not necessarily your truth. If your brain can make up a compelling reason it will feel like truth, even if it isn’t. It is crucial to understand this before you go telling people your stories. Speak to your senses and what you’re feeling and the context you’re feeling them in. Talk about the experience you are having in an open-ended, sensory-based way.

## Identifying Purpose and Self-Care

I want a wonder-filled life. I want to create it, as much as possible, for myself. I want people and places, things and experiences that help me grow into a better person to surround me. Focusing on how time and sensation flows allows me to focus less on my capacity and more on my senses. It allows me to remain focused on the abundance I can create within my life.

Choosing myself requires me to get to know myself. It requires imagining new ways of being. It requires me to regularly evaluate and reform the relationships, the agreements, I have with other people, with the objects I surround myself with, and with the experiences I co-create.

Choosing myself, loving myself, cherishing myself requires that I spend time alone with my thoughts and with my body. I make myself accountable to my values and keep myself psychologically and physiologically safe when I do this.

I am allowed to constantly rewrite the narrative I hold around who I am based on the changing context of my life. We all are. Consent can be given or taken, allowed, disallowed or accepted — at any point in time within the infinite game of life.
